# How to Convert a USD Budget into a Competitive Italian Gross Salary

> Work backwards from a USD budget to an Italian gross salary offer. Learn how IRPEF and INPS shape take-home pay so you neither overpay nor underbid.



You have a budget in dollars. Your candidate thinks in euros, and in net monthly pay. The offer you write must be a gross annual figure in euros. Getting from one to the other is where most US teams go wrong.

This guide works backwards from the budget. It shows what sits between a gross offer and what the person actually receives.

## Start with the right question

Most founders ask: "What salary can we afford?" That is only half of it. Two numbers matter:

- **Total employer cost.** This is your budget ceiling. It includes the gross salary plus employer-side contributions and other mandatory costs.
- **Net pay to the employee.** This is what the candidate compares against other offers.

The gross salary sits between the two. Your job is to pick a gross figure where the total cost fits your budget and the net pay is competitive.

## Step one: fix the budget in euros

Convert your USD budget to euros at a rate you can defend. Use a recent rate and add a margin for movement. A budget that works at today's rate may not work after a currency swing. If your cost base is in dollars, build a buffer into the offer instead of renegotiating later.

Then subtract the employer-side costs to find the gross salary your budget can carry. Employer contributions are paid on top of gross pay. They are not part of it. Many first-time Italian employers forget this and treat the budget as if it were the gross figure. That is how teams end up overpaying or running over budget.

Your cost calculator will do this step for you. The point here is to see the logic: budget, minus employer costs, equals the maximum gross.

## Step two: see what the employee loses

A candidate will not judge your gross figure in isolation. They will estimate what lands in their bank account. Two deductions drive that.

**Social security.** The employee's share of INPS pension contributions (IVS) is 9.19% of the taxable base. It is withheld from the gross salary every month.

**Income tax.** Italy taxes personal income under IRPEF, with rising brackets:

| Annual income (EUR) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to 28,000 | 23% |
| 28,000 to 50,000 | 33% |
| Above 50,000 | 43% |

These are marginal rates. Each rate applies only to the slice of income inside that bracket. A person earning above 50,000 does not pay 43% on everything. They pay 23% on the first slice, 33% on the next, and 43% only on the part above 50,000.

Local surtaxes and any deductions also apply, so treat the brackets as the core of the estimate, not the whole of it.

## Why the brackets matter for your offer

The jump from 33% to 43% changes how much an extra euro of gross is worth to the candidate. Once a salary is in the top bracket, each additional euro of gross delivers less net than it did lower down. That has two practical effects.

First, a small gross raise near a bracket line buys little net pay for the employee. It still costs you the full amount plus employer contributions. Pushing the offer across a threshold is often poor value.

Second, a gross figure just below a threshold can be more competitive than it looks. The candidate keeps a larger share of each euro.

Before you commit, run your target gross through the brackets and see where it falls.

## Avoid the two common mistakes

**Overpaying.** This happens when a US team converts a dollar salary directly into euros and calls it the gross. Italian gross pay is usually not a one-to-one match with a US base salary. Employer contributions and the tax structure change the picture. A direct conversion can leave you well above the market rate for the role, or well above what your budget can carry.

**Underbidding.** This happens when a team quotes a gross that looks fine in dollars but delivers a thin net after INPS and IRPEF. The candidate runs the numbers, sees the take-home, and walks. They compare net, not gross.

## A simple working method

1. Set the total budget and convert it to euros with a safety margin.
2. Subtract employer-side costs to find the maximum gross.
3. Apply the employee's INPS share of 9.19% to that gross.
4. Run the remaining taxable amount through the IRPEF brackets.
5. Read the resulting net pay against the market for the role.
6. Adjust the gross up or down until cost and net both make sense.

If the net is too low to compete, you have three honest options. Raise the budget, narrow the role, or offer non-cash value the candidate cares about. Do not hide the gap with a gross number that looks good on paper.

## Keep the offer honest

Share the gross figure in the offer, and be ready to explain the net. Candidates in Italy expect to talk about take-home pay. A clear, accurate estimate builds trust early and avoids surprises on the first payslip.

Verify the rates before you finalize any offer, since tax and contribution rules can change. The figures above come from published secondary sources, not from a payroll run.
