# Hiring in Italy: EOR, Contractors or Your Own Entity?

> A plain decision matrix for US teams hiring in Italy. Compare EOR, contractors and your own entity by headcount, risk and speed.



You want to hire in Italy. You have a model for the US, but Italy works differently. The question is which setup fits your situation today, and what it will cost you to change later.

This guide compares the main routes: an Employer of Record (EOR), independent contractors, and your own Italian entity. It uses headcount, risk and speed as the lenses. It skips the legal detail you can get from an advisor once you pick a direction.

## The routes in plain terms

**Employer of Record (EOR).** A local provider becomes the legal employer. You direct the daily work. The EOR runs payroll, contracts, social security and compliance. Your hire is a real employee under Italian rules.

**Contractors.** You engage an individual as a self-employed professional. They invoice you. You do not run payroll. You also do not control their hours and methods the way you do with staff.

**Your own entity.** You register a company in Italy. You become the employer directly. You need local accounting, payroll, a registered address and ongoing filings.

## Decision matrix

| | EOR | Contractor | Own entity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best headcount | A handful of hires | Short, defined projects | A larger, stable team |
| Risk | Low for you, carried mostly by the EOR | Highest if the person works like an employee | Full risk sits with you |
| Speed to start | Fast | Fastest | Slowest |
| Setup effort | Minimal | Minimal | Heavy |
| Control over the person | Full day-to-day direction | Limited by design | Full |
| Exit flexibility | High | High | Low |

Read the table by row, not by column. The right choice usually depends on which row hurts most.

## Headcount

Headcount is the first filter, but not the only one.

For a single hire or a very small team, your own entity rarely makes sense. The fixed work of running a local company is the same whether you employ a few people or many. Spread over a small team, it is expensive in money and attention.

As the team grows, the math shifts. An entity has setup effort, but its running cost per head falls as you add people. An EOR fee is closer to a per-person cost. At some point, the entity wins on cost. That point depends on your salaries, your plans and how long you commit.

Contractors do not follow this curve. They fit a different need: a bounded task, not a team member.

## Risk

Risk is where founders get surprised.

Contractors look cheap and simple. The danger is misclassification. If the person works fixed hours, uses your tools, reports to your manager and works only for you, authorities may treat them as an employee. That can bring back-payments and penalties. If the role looks like a job, treat it as a job.

An EOR moves most of the employment compliance to a provider that does this daily. You still own your management choices. You do not own the paperwork.

With your own entity, every obligation is yours. That is fine when you have local finance and HR support. It is a poor fit if you are learning the system as you go.

## Speed

Speed matters when you have a candidate who may accept another offer.

Contractors are the quickest to start, but speed is a weak reason to pick them if the role is a real job.

An EOR is usually the fastest route to a compliant employee. The provider already has the structures in place. You sign, they onboard, your hire starts.

An entity takes the longest. You must form it, open accounts, register for payroll and set up local providers before the first person starts. Plan for that time before you promise a start date.

## Which one to choose

Use these rules of thumb.

- **Testing the market, first hires:** choose an EOR. You learn the market without building infrastructure.
- **A specific project with a clear end:** a contractor can work, if the person truly runs their own work.
- **A long-term team with steady growth:** plan for your own entity, and consider an EOR as the bridge while you build it.
- **Key role, need to move fast:** choose an EOR.
- **Unclear role boundaries:** do not use a contractor. Hire properly.

## A common path

Many US companies do not pick just one. They start with an EOR for the first hires. They use contractors for short, separate tasks. When the team is large enough and the market is proven, they open an entity and move staff across.

This staged approach limits upfront cost and keeps you flexible. It also gives you real data on what the Italian team costs and delivers before you commit to a structure.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Will this person act like a team member? If yes, avoid the contractor route.
- How long will you stay in Italy? A short horizon favors EOR or contractors.
- Do you have local finance and HR help? If not, avoid an entity for now.
- How quickly do you need a start date? If soon, an EOR is the safest fast route.

Pick the route that matches where you are today. You can change it as the team grows.
