# Electronic vs Paper Meal Vouchers in Italy: Which Should You Offer?

> Electronic meal vouchers carry a higher daily tax-free limit than paper ones in Italy. See how the two formats compare on tax treatment and employee preference.



Meal vouchers, called *buoni pasto*, are one of the most common benefits in Italian employment. Many employees expect them. If you hire in Italy, you will need to decide which format to offer: paper or electronic.

The choice affects your payroll cost and how your team feels about the benefit. This article compares the two on tax treatment and employee preference.

## Why the format matters

In Italy, meal vouchers are not treated like ordinary salary. Up to a daily limit, the value is usually exempt from income tax and social contributions. That makes them an efficient way to give employees more value than the same amount paid as cash wages.

The exemption has a ceiling. Anything above the daily limit is treated as normal taxable pay. The ceiling is different for each format.

## Tax treatment: the daily limits

According to current reporting on the latest budget law, the daily limit for electronic vouchers is 10.0 EUR per day. For paper vouchers it is 4.0 EUR per day.

The gap is large. Electronic vouchers allow more than double the tax-free value per working day. The source is a secondary one, so check the figures with your payroll provider before you set up a plan.

Here is what that means in practice:

- **Electronic:** you can give a higher daily value before any tax or contribution applies.
- **Paper:** the tax-free room is much smaller. A voucher worth more than the limit pushes the excess into taxable pay.

For a finance team, the point is simple. The same benefit budget goes further with electronic vouchers. You pay less in employer contributions on the benefit, and the employee keeps more net value.

## Employee preference

We do not have survey data to cite here, so this section sticks to what is easy to observe.

Employees generally like electronic vouchers because they are easy to use. The balance sits on a card or an app. There is nothing to carry, count or lose. Many workers also value being able to see the remaining balance at any time.

Paper vouchers have some friction. Employees receive booklets and hand them over at the till. Some shops limit how many vouchers they accept in one purchase, and some do not take them at all. Lost or damaged vouchers are hard to replace.

There is one point in favor of paper. Some older or less digital workers are used to it and find it familiar. If your team is small and local, this may matter.

For most foreign companies hiring remote or hybrid staff, electronic is the easier fit. It works for people who split their time between home and office, and it requires no physical distribution.

## Admin and operations

Electronic vouchers are also simpler for you to run.

- **No printing or shipping.** Balances are loaded digitally each month.
- **Cleaner records.** The provider reports usage, which helps your payroll and finance teams.
- **Easier for remote staff.** You do not need to send anything to an employee's home.

Paper vouchers need physical handling. Someone has to order, deliver and track them. For a company without a local office, that is extra work with little benefit.

## Which one should you offer?

For most US companies hiring in Italy, electronic vouchers are the better default. They have a higher tax-free limit, they are easier for employees to use, and they take less effort to manage.

Paper may still make sense in a narrow case. For example, you might inherit an existing arrangement, or your workforce may have a clear preference for it. Even then, the lower limit means less tax-free value per day.

## Before you decide

Three checks will keep you out of trouble:

1. Confirm the current daily limits with your payroll advisor. Rules can change with each budget law.
2. Check whether your collective agreement sets a minimum voucher value or eligibility rules.
3. Decide whether vouchers are part of the base offer or an addition. Put it in writing in the employment contract or a company policy.

Meal vouchers are a small line in the offer letter, but they shape how competitive your package looks to Italian candidates. Getting the format right costs nothing and saves tax.
