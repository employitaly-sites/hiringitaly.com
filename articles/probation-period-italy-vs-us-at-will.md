# Probation Periods in Italian Employment Contracts: How They Differ From At-Will

> How the trial period works in Italy, what it requires in writing, and why it is not a substitute for US-style at-will termination.



US founders often ask the same question when they plan a hire in Italy. Can we just use a trial period and let the person go if it does not work out? The short answer is that the trial period helps, but it is not at-will employment. Here is how it works and where the gap is.

## What a probation period is in Italy

A probation period (periodo di prova) is an early phase of an open-ended employment contract. During it, both sides can test the fit. The employee sees the job and the company. The employer sees whether the person can do the work.

The employee is a full employee from day one. Pay, social contributions, leave accrual and health and safety rules all apply. Probation does not create a lighter category of worker.

## It must be agreed in writing

This is the rule that catches foreign employers most often. Probation exists only if the written contract says so, and it must be signed before the employee starts work. If the clause is missing, or added after the start date, the probation is generally treated as void. The employee is then covered by the full dismissal protections from the start.

The clause should also describe the duties that will be tested. A vague clause that names no tasks is open to challenge. If the role changes after hiring, the clause may not cover the new duties.

## How long it can last

The length is not free. National law sets an outer limit, and the applicable collective agreement (CCNL) usually sets the working limit for each job level. Senior roles tend to get longer periods than junior ones. The contract cannot go beyond what the collective agreement allows.

Check the CCNL that applies to your sector before you draft the clause. The same job title can carry a different maximum under a different agreement.

## Ending the contract during probation

This is where probation looks closest to at-will. During the trial, either side can generally end the contract without the notice period that would apply later. The employer does not need to prove just cause or a justified reason in the usual way.

That said, it is not a blank check. A few limits matter in practice:

- The employer cannot end the contract for a discriminatory reason or in retaliation.
- The trial must be real. If the employee was never given a fair chance to perform the tasks named in the contract, a court may find the termination unjustified.
- Protected situations, such as pregnancy or illness, can limit the employer's freedom to terminate.
- The employee must actually be allowed to do the work. Leaving someone idle and then ending the trial invites a dispute.

In most cases, a clear and documented termination within the probation window is low risk. The risk rises when the reason looks unrelated to performance.

## What happens when probation ends

If neither side ends the contract by the last day of the trial, the employment confirms automatically. Time spent in probation counts toward length of service. From that point, the full Italian dismissal framework applies. Ending the relationship now needs a lawful reason, a proper procedure and, in many cases, notice. This is the real difference from the US.

In the US, at-will is the default for the whole relationship. In Italy, flexibility is front-loaded. It sits in the probation window and then mostly disappears.

## How it differs from US expectations

Three points deserve attention from HR, finance and legal ops teams.

**The default is reversed.** In the US, you need a reason to restrict termination, such as a contract. In Italy, you need a reason to terminate, except in the probation window.

**Paperwork decides the outcome.** A missing signature or a late clause can erase the protection you thought you had. Treat the contract as a compliance document, not a formality.

**Probation is a decision point, not a formality.** Teams should schedule a real review before the window closes. Waiting until the last days leaves little room to act, and silence confirms the hire.

## Practical steps for a foreign employer

- Identify the CCNL that applies to the role before you draft.
- Put the probation clause in the signed contract before day one of work.
- List the duties that the trial will cover.
- Give the employee real work and real feedback during the period.
- Hold a documented review well before the end date.
- Decide in time, because the contract confirms by default.

If you hire through an Employer of Record (EOR), the EOR is the legal employer and handles the contract, the clause and the CCNL choice. You still manage the work and the evaluation. The decision on the trial is a joint one in practice, so agree on the review timeline at the start.

## The bottom line

Probation in Italy gives you a window of real flexibility, but only if you set it up correctly and use it on time. After the window closes, you are no longer in at-will territory. Plan the hire with that in mind.
