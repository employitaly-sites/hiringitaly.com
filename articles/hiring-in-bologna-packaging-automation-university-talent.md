# Hiring in Bologna: Packaging, Automation and University Talent

> A city profile of Bologna for US employers: packaging and automation clusters, university talent, and the local payroll tax detail to budget for.



Bologna is a mid-sized city with an outsized industrial base. If you build machines, software for machines, or products that need to be packed and shipped, it deserves a place on your shortlist. This profile covers the clusters, the talent pipeline, and one local cost line to plan for.

## Why Bologna

Bologna sits at the center of Emilia-Romagna. The region is one of Italy's strongest manufacturing areas. The city works as its commercial and academic hub. Rail and road links are good, and the airport serves European routes.

For a US company, the appeal is simple. You get deep technical know-how in a place where engineers, machinists and researchers live within a short distance of each other.

## The packaging cluster

The area around Bologna is known for packaging machinery. Companies here design and build lines that fill, wrap, label and box products. Food, beverage, pharmaceutical and cosmetics makers buy them worldwide.

This matters for hiring in three ways.

- Mechanical and mechatronic engineers are easy to find, and many have worked on export projects.
- Service and commissioning technicians are used to traveling to client sites abroad.
- Suppliers of components, tooling and controls sit nearby, so people move between firms and share skills.

If you are opening a sales or support office for packaging equipment, you will find candidates who already speak the language of your buyers.

## Automation and mechatronics

Packaging machines are only one part of the picture. The same region has a long tradition in automatic machines, motion control, robotics and industrial software. A packaging line is a mix of mechanics, electronics and code. That mix produced a labor market full of people who can work across all three.

Roles you can realistically fill in Bologna include:

- Controls and PLC engineers
- Embedded software developers
- Robotics and vision specialists
- Industrial design and CAD engineers
- Technical sales engineers
- Field service and after-sales support

English is common among engineers who work with foreign clients. It is less common in support and administrative roles. Plan for that when you write job ads and run interviews.

## University talent

The University of Bologna is among the oldest in the world, and it feeds the city's talent pool every year. Engineering, computer science, economics, law and statistics graduates all enter the local market. Many students come from other Italian regions and stay after graduation because of the job market.

The university also connects to industry through research projects, internships and thesis work with local companies. If you want junior hires, internships are a practical way to meet candidates before you make an offer. A student who has already worked on a real machine or dataset needs less onboarding.

For senior hires, the local cluster is the better source. Experienced engineers often move between machine builders, and they know the competitors' products.

## A local cost line: the municipal surtax

Italian employees pay a municipal income tax surtax on top of national income tax. It varies by city. According to a published summary of municipal surtaxes in provincial capitals, Bologna applies a single rate of 0.8%, with an exemption for income up to EUR 15,000.

This is withheld from the employee's pay, not added on top of your cost. Still, it affects net salary. When you compare a gross offer in Bologna with one in another city, the net figure the candidate sees can differ. Candidates compare net pay, so check it before you finalize an offer.

The source is a secondary one. Confirm the current rate and threshold with your payroll provider before you rely on them.

## How to hire without a local entity

You do not need an Italian company to employ someone in Bologna. An Employer of Record (EOR) hires the person on your behalf, runs payroll, handles social contributions and issues compliant contracts. You direct the work. The EOR carries the employer obligations.

This suits a first hire or a small team. You can test the market, see whether the local talent fits your product, and decide later whether to open an entity. If you hire non-EU staff, an EOR can also help with visas and relocation.

## What to check before you start

- Which collective agreement covers the role. It sets minimum pay, notice periods and extra pay items.
- Total employer cost, not just gross salary. Social contributions add a significant amount on top.
- Where the employee will actually work. Remote work from another city can change local tax details.
- Language needs for the role, both for daily work and for customers.

Bologna rewards companies that need hands-on technical depth. The packaging and automation base is real, the university keeps supplying graduates, and the city is easy to reach from the rest of Europe.
