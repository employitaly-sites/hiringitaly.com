# Hiring in Turin: Automotive, Aerospace and Engineering Talent

> A city profile of Turin for US employers hiring engineers: the talent base, how to hire, and what the municipal income tax surcharge means for payroll.



Turin is Italy's industrial engineering city. If you build vehicles, aircraft components, embedded software or manufacturing systems, you will find people here who have spent their careers on exactly that work. This profile covers who you can hire, how the hire works for a foreign company, and one local payroll detail that often surprises finance teams: the municipal surcharge.

## The talent base

The city grew around the car industry, and the supply chain that formed around it is still there. Mechanical, electrical and manufacturing engineers are the core of the market. Over time the same skills spread into aerospace, where precision machining, systems integration and testing matter just as much.

The local technical university is a steady source of graduates in engineering, design and computing. Many of them stay in the area. That helps if you need junior engineers, and it helps if you want to build a team around a few senior hires.

Common profiles you can expect to find:

- Powertrain, chassis and body engineers
- Electrification and battery specialists
- Embedded software and controls engineers
- Aerospace structures and systems engineers
- Quality, validation and manufacturing engineers
- Industrial designers and CAD specialists

The market is mature. Candidates know how supplier relationships work, they are used to working with international customers, and many read and write technical English. Do not assume that every candidate is comfortable negotiating in English. For senior roles, expect to run part of the process in Italian or to have a local adviser in the room.

## How hiring works for a foreign company

You do not need your own Italian entity to hire in Turin. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person on your behalf under Italian law. You direct the daily work. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social security filings and the statutory paperwork.

For engineering roles, two points matter early.

First, the collective agreement. Most engineering and metalworking roles fall under a national collective agreement (CCNL). It sets minimum pay, working hours, notice periods and extra benefits. Picking the right agreement is the first step, because everything else in the contract follows from it.

Second, intellectual property. If your engineers will create designs or code, the contract needs clear language on who owns the output. Italian rules on employee inventions are specific. Put this in writing before the start date, not after the first prototype.

## The local surcharge

Italy taxes employee income at national and regional level. On top of that, each municipality can add its own surcharge on personal income tax. Turin applies one.

According to a secondary source, the Turin surcharge uses four brackets, with rates of 0.8%, 0.8%, 1.1% and 1.2%. The same source reports an exemption for income up to EUR 11,790. Treat these as figures to confirm against the municipality's current rules before you finalize a budget.

Three practical points follow.

**It is the employee's tax, not an extra employer cost.** The surcharge is calculated on the employee's income and withheld through payroll. It does not add a line to the employer's contribution bill. It does reduce the employee's net pay, so it affects what a given gross salary feels like to the candidate.

**It varies by city.** A candidate in Turin and a candidate in another Italian city, on the same gross salary, can take home slightly different amounts. If you compare locations, compare net pay, not only gross.

**It rises with income.** The top bracket rate is higher than the first. For senior engineers on higher salaries, the surcharge is a small but real part of the gap between gross and net. Salary offers that are built on a net target should account for it.

The amounts are modest next to income tax and social contributions. They still belong in your model, especially when you give candidates a net figure in a first call.

## What to plan for

Before you open a role in Turin, settle these items:

1. The collective agreement that fits the role.
2. The gross salary, and the net figure the candidate will see.
3. The IP and confidentiality terms.
4. Where the person will work: office, plant, client site or home.
5. Who manages them day to day, and in which time zone.

Engineering candidates in Turin often compare offers from large manufacturers, which offer stability and structured benefits. A small foreign team needs to be clear about what it offers in return: scope, technical ownership and a direct line to decisions.

## Summary

Turin gives you a deep pool of automotive, aerospace and engineering talent, and a hiring path that does not require a local company. The municipal surcharge is a small line, but it changes the net pay your candidate sees. Build it into your numbers early.
