# Italian Employment Contract Checklist: What to Include Before a Hire Signs

> A plain checklist of the mandatory and recommended clauses in an Italian employment contract, for US founders, HR, finance and legal ops teams.



An Italian employment contract is not a blank page. The national collective agreement (CCNL) for your sector sets many terms before you write a word. Your contract has to sit on top of it. This checklist covers what must be in the contract and what you should add.

## Start with the collective agreement

Pick the right CCNL first. It sets minimum pay levels, job grades, working hours, leave, notice periods and often extra pay such as a thirteenth month. Many US teams skip this step and draft from a US template. That is the most common mistake.

Name the CCNL in the contract. Name the grade and level too. If the contract and the CCNL conflict, the more favorable term for the employee usually wins.

## Mandatory content

Italian law requires the employer to give the employee written information about the main terms of the job. Put all of the following in the contract itself so nothing depends on a separate notice.

- **Parties.** Full legal details of the employer and the employee.
- **Place of work.** The office address, or a clear statement that work is remote or mobile.
- **Start date.** And whether the contract is permanent or fixed-term. If fixed-term, state the end date and the reason where the rules require one.
- **Job title, grade and duties.** Use the CCNL grade. The grade drives pay, and it is hard to change later without consent.
- **Probation period.** If you want one, it must be in writing before work starts. Its length is capped by the CCNL and by law.
- **Pay.** Gross base salary, how often it is paid, and every pay element: allowances, bonuses, extra monthly payments.
- **Working hours.** Full-time or part-time, the weekly schedule, and rules on overtime.
- **Paid leave.** Annual holiday and other leave as set by law and the CCNL.
- **Notice.** The notice period for both sides, as set by the CCNL.
- **Collective agreement.** The CCNL that applies, as noted above.
- **Social security and insurance.** The institutions that receive contributions, and the severance fund arrangement (TFR).

A contract that is not in writing, or that leaves these items out, invites disputes and possible penalties. Do not rely on a verbal offer.

## Recommended clauses

These are not always required. They protect both sides and avoid arguments later.

**Confidentiality.** Define what is confidential and that the duty continues after the job ends.

**Intellectual property.** State that work product belongs to the employer. For inventions, Italian rules give the employee certain rights, so have a local lawyer check the wording.

**Exclusivity.** If you need the employee to work only for you, say so. Without a clause, moonlighting rules are limited.

**Non-compete.** Italian non-competes are enforceable only if they meet strict conditions. They need a defined scope, a time limit, a geographic limit and separate payment. A copy-paste US clause will not hold.

**Remote work.** Set out where the employee may work, what equipment you provide, and how you reach them. Cover data security and working hours.

**Company equipment.** Laptop, phone and software: who owns them, how they may be used, and when they are returned.

**Data protection.** Include the privacy notice for employee data. Italy applies EU data rules, and monitoring staff is tightly restricted.

**Company policies.** Reference the code of conduct, IT policy and disciplinary code. Employees should receive them and confirm receipt.

**Expenses and travel.** Say what you reimburse and how to claim.

## Language and format

Write the contract in Italian. Add an English version for your own team if it helps. State which version prevails. If the employee does not read Italian well, a bilingual contract reduces the risk of a later claim that they did not understand it.

## What not to copy from the US

- **At-will language.** It does not exist in Italy. Dismissal needs a valid reason and a set procedure.
- **Broad termination clauses.** You cannot contract out of the dismissal rules.
- **Unpaid bonus promises.** Anything you pay regularly can become part of fixed pay. Be exact about what is discretionary.
- **Vague titles.** A loose title can lead to a claim for a higher grade.

## Before signing

Run a short final check.

1. Does the contract name the correct CCNL, grade and level?
2. Are probation, notice and pay stated exactly as the CCNL allows?
3. Is every pay element written down?
4. Are the non-compete and IP clauses reviewed by an Italian lawyer?
5. Has the employee received the privacy notice and company policies?
6. Is the contract registered with the authorities as required before the start date?

If you do not have an Italian entity, an Employer of Record can issue the contract under its own entity. It then handles registration, payroll and contributions. You still direct the daily work. The contract checklist above applies either way.
