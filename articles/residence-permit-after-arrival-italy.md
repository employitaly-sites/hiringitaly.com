# The Residence Permit After Arrival: Your Employee's First Weeks in Italy

> The visa gets your hire into Italy. The permesso di soggiorno lets them stay and work. Here is what happens next and what your team should track.



The visa gets your new employee into Italy. It does not let them stay. The residence permit, called the *permesso di soggiorno*, does that. Most delays in a relocation happen in the weeks right after landing, not before.

This guide walks through what happens next and what your HR and legal ops teams should watch.

## The visa is an entry document

A work visa lets your employee enter Italy for the purpose of working. Once they arrive, they must convert that entry into a permit to stay. The permit is a separate document, issued by the local police headquarters (the *questura*) after an application.

The steps start almost immediately. Deadlines after arrival are short and strict. Confirm the exact window for your employee's case before they travel, and tell them on day one.

## Step 1: Declare presence

A person arriving from outside the Schengen area must declare their presence in Italy. In most cases the border stamp or a form handled by the hotel or host covers this. Keep a copy. The questura may ask for it later.

## Step 2: Get a tax code

The *codice fiscale* is the Italian tax ID. Your employee needs it for the employment contract, payroll, a bank account, a lease and a phone plan. Often it is requested before arrival or issued at the first appointment. If it is missing, payroll cannot start properly.

## Step 3: Sign the stay contract

For most work visas, the employee and the employer must meet at the Immigration One-Stop Shop (*Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione*) at the local prefecture. There, they sign the *contratto di soggiorno*. This document ties the permit to the job.

Your employer of record usually handles the employer side of this. The employee must attend in person with the right documents. Plan the trip so nobody is traveling when the appointment falls.

## Step 4: File the permit application

The permit application is submitted through a postal kit, available at designated Post Office counters. The kit has forms, a copy of the passport and visa, and proof of the stay contract. The employee fills it in and returns it at the counter.

The post office gives a receipt. Keep it safe. It is the proof that the application is pending.

Mistakes here cost weeks. Common errors are:

- Names that do not match the passport exactly.
- A missing signature.
- An address the employee cannot use for mail.

Have someone who knows the process review the kit before submission.

## Step 5: Attend the questura appointment

After filing, the questura calls the employee in. They give fingerprints and a photo. They also hand over or confirm the details of the application. Wait times depend heavily on the city. Some offices are quick. Others are slow.

The employee should check their mail and phone often. A missed appointment can reset the clock.

## Can they start working before the permit arrives?

In many work cases, the postal receipt allows the employee to start work while the permit is being produced. This is a common point of confusion. Do not guess. Have your provider confirm that the receipt is enough for your specific visa category before the first day of work.

## Step 6: Register residence and healthcare

Once settled, the employee registers their address with the local town hall (*anagrafe*). This is the residence registration. It matters for healthcare, a driving license, tax and many daily tasks.

They can then enrol in the national health service. Some employees also want private cover during the first weeks. Ask your benefits team what is included.

## What the employer must do

Your company is not a bystander. In the first weeks, expect to:

- Communicate the new hire to the authorities, as required for any employee in Italy.
- Provide the documents needed for the stay contract.
- Give the employee a stable address for mail, or help find housing quickly.
- Keep a record of the receipt, the permit and its expiry.
- Set a reminder well before the permit expires. Renewal should start early.

An employer of record can run this checklist for you. You still need to know what is happening, since it affects start dates and onboarding.

## Practical tips for founders and HR

- **Do not book the start date too tightly.** Leave room for appointments and mail delays.
- **Assign one contact.** The employee will have questions in a new language and a new system. One person at your provider should own them.
- **Share a plain timeline.** A one-page list of steps and who does what removes most anxiety.
- **Keep copies of everything.** Passport, visa, receipt, stay contract, tax code, residence registration.
- **Plan for family.** If relatives join, they have their own paperwork and timelines.

## The short version

The visa opens the door. After that, the employee must declare presence, get a tax code, sign the stay contract, file the postal kit, attend the questura, and register their residence. Each step has a deadline and a document. Miss one and onboarding slips.

Good planning turns the first weeks from stressful to routine.
