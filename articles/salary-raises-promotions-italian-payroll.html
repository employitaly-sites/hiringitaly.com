How to Handle Salary Raises and Promotions on Italian Payroll

A raise in Italy is not just a number change in your HR system. It touches the employment contract, the collective agreement, payroll and sometimes the employee’s job level. If you skip a step, you get payslip errors and disputes later.

This guide walks through the process in the order it should happen.

Start with the decision, in writing  

Before anything reaches payroll, record the approval. Keep it simple and internal:

  • The employee’s name and current role.
  • The new gross salary, or the new role and the salary that goes with it.
  • The effective date.
  • Who approved it and why.

The effective date matters more than most teams expect. Payroll is built around a monthly cycle. A change agreed late in the month, but meant to apply earlier, forces a manual adjustment. Decide the effective date with the payroll calendar in mind.

Check the collective agreement  

Most Italian employees are covered by a national collective agreement. It sets minimum pay for each job level and defines what each level means. This is where a promotion differs from a plain raise.

A raise keeps the same role and level. The pay goes up, but the contract terms otherwise stay put.

A promotion changes the duties, and often the contractual level. The new level has its own minimum pay, its own description and sometimes different rules on notice or other terms. Check that the new salary does not fall below the minimum for the new level.

Also check whether the employee already receives pay above the minimum. Some companies treat that extra amount as absorbable, meaning a later increase can be set against it. Others do not. If your original contract says nothing, ask your payroll provider how it has been treated so far. Do not assume.

Decide how the raise is structured  

A raise can take different forms. The most common are an increase in base pay or a separate fixed allowance. Each has a different effect on other items calculated from base pay, such as severance accrual and some paid leave calculations.

This is why the structure should be chosen deliberately, not left to whoever updates the spreadsheet. Ask your payroll provider or labor consultant to confirm what the structure changes downstream before you commit.

Prepare the contract addendum  

In Italy, a change to the terms of employment should be documented. For a raise or promotion, that usually means a short addendum to the employment contract, or a letter of variation signed by both sides.

A good addendum states:

  • The new gross salary and how it is structured.
  • The new role and level, if there is a promotion.
  • The effective date.
  • That all other terms remain unchanged.

Write it in Italian, and add an English version for the employee if useful. Have a local advisor review it. The signed document goes into the employee file and a copy goes to payroll.

Do not rely on an email thread as the only record. Emails get lost, and they rarely carry the detail a payroll audit or a later dispute will ask for.

Send the data to payroll on time  

Payroll closes before the payslip is issued. Your provider will have a cutoff for changes. If the addendum and the new figures arrive after that cutoff, the change slips to the next cycle, and the difference has to be paid as a catch-up.

Send payroll these items together:

  • The signed addendum.
  • The effective date.
  • The new level and job title, if any.
  • Any change to allowances or other recurring items.

Sending everything in one package avoids the back and forth that causes most delays.

Review the first payslip carefully  

When the first payslip with the change arrives, check it against the addendum. Look at:

  • Gross pay matches the agreed figure.
  • The job level and title are correct.
  • Any retroactive amount is shown and explained.
  • Net pay looks reasonable given the change in taxes and social contributions.

A higher gross salary does not raise net pay by the same amount. Tax and contributions take a larger share as pay rises. Tell the employee this before the payslip arrives, so the net figure is not a surprise.

Update your own records  

After the payslip checks out, update your internal systems: HR records, budget forecasts and any cost reports. If you use a headcount or cost model for Italy, refresh it with the new salary. Employer contributions and accruals move with the salary, so the true cost of the raise is higher than the gross increase alone.

A short checklist  

  • Approve in writing, with an effective date.
  • Check the collective agreement and the level.
  • Choose the pay structure on purpose.
  • Draft and sign the addendum.
  • Send one complete package to payroll before the cutoff.
  • Verify the first payslip.
  • Update your cost forecast.

Done in this order, a raise or promotion is routine. Done out of order, it creates corrections that take weeks to clear.
