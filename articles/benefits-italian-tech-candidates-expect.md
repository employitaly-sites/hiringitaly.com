# Benefits Italian Tech Candidates Expect Beyond Salary

> What Italian tech candidates expect beyond salary, by seniority, and how US employers can build a benefits package that fits the local market.



Salary gets the first call. Benefits decide the second one.

US founders hiring in Italy often assume a strong base pay closes the deal. It rarely does on its own. Italian tech candidates compare the whole package. What they expect depends a lot on seniority.

## What everyone expects

Some things are baseline. Candidates at every level assume them. Missing them signals that you do not know the market.

- A proper written contract with clear terms, role and reporting line.
- The statutory protections and paid leave that come with Italian employment.
- Social security and pension contributions paid correctly and on time.
- A clear notice and probation framework.

These are not perks. They are the floor. If you hire through an Employer of Record (EOR), they come built in, which removes one source of doubt for candidates who have never heard of your company.

## Junior candidates: stability and learning

Junior engineers, analysts and support staff want two things above the rest: security and growth.

Many are early in their careers and have seen friends move between short contracts. A permanent contract from a foreign company matters more to them than a perk list. They also look for structured learning.

What works for this group:

- A permanent contract, not a chain of short ones.
- A named mentor and a clear first-year plan.
- A training budget they can actually use, for courses, books and conferences.
- Meal vouchers or a similar daily benefit, which are common and easy to compare.
- Flexible hours, even if the role is mostly on site.

Juniors rarely negotiate hard. They do compare offers closely, and small, concrete benefits tip the choice.

## Mid-level candidates: flexibility and health

Mid-level people have options and know it. They often have a family, a mortgage or a partner with a job in the same city. Their questions shift from learning to life fit.

What they ask about:

- Remote or hybrid work, and whether it is a real policy or a favor.
- Supplementary health insurance, ideally covering family members.
- Flexible working hours and time off beyond the legal minimum.
- Equipment, a home office allowance and coworking access.
- A clear path to a senior title.

Supplementary health cover carries real weight here. Italy has public healthcare, but many professionals still want private options for speed and choice. A plan that covers family is valued more than a small salary bump.

Remote work is the other big filter. Candidates will walk away from a role that demands daily office presence in a city they do not want to live in. If your team is fully remote, say so early.

## Senior candidates: total package and autonomy

Senior engineers, managers and specialists judge the offer as a whole. They want to see how each part is structured, not only the headline number.

What matters to them:

- Equity or a long-term incentive, explained in plain terms, with clear tax treatment.
- A bonus with transparent rules, not a vague promise.
- Supplementary pension contributions.
- Autonomy over how and where they work.
- A seat at the table on product and technical decisions.
- Support for conferences, speaking and open source work.

Equity is where US employers most often stumble. A plan written for US employees may not translate cleanly to an Italian employment relationship. Seniors know this. If you cannot explain how it works for them in Italy, they will discount it to zero. Get local advice before you put it in an offer.

## Relocation and international hires

Some of your best candidates may not live in Italy yet. Others may be Italians returning from abroad. Both groups ask about practical support.

Expect questions about:

- Help with housing in the first months.
- Visa and residence paperwork for non-EU hires.
- Registration with local services.
- Tax and social security status in the first year.

A clear answer here builds trust fast. Vague promises do the opposite.

## How to build the package

Do not copy a US benefits plan. Do not try to match every perk a large local employer offers either. Pick a few that fit your team and do them well.

1. Start with the legal floor and make sure it is correct.
2. Add the daily benefits candidates compare most, such as meal vouchers and flexible hours.
3. Offer health cover that includes family if your budget allows.
4. Be explicit about remote and hybrid rules.
5. For senior roles, put the incentive structure in writing and have it reviewed locally.

Then tell candidates about it. Many companies offer decent benefits and hide them in a long document. Put the package in the first conversation, not the last.

## The cost side

Every benefit has a price, and the employer cost in Italy is higher than the salary line. Contributions and mandatory items add on top. Before you promise anything, model the full cost per hire, so the package you offer is one you can sustain.
