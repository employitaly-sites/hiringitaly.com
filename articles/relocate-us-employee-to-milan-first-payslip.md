# Relocating a US Employee to Milan: From Decision to First Payslip

> Follow a US hire from the decision to move to Milan through to the first Italian payslip, including the Milan local income tax surcharge.



A US software company decides to send a senior engineer to Milan. The engineer, call her Dana, wants to stay on the same team. The company has no Italian entity. Finance asks a fair question: what happens between the decision and the first payslip?

This is the path, step by step.

## The decision

The team agrees on the role, the start date and the pay. At this stage, most founders think about salary and housing. The better question is who will be the employer in Italy.

Dana will live and work in Milan. Italian rules apply to her employment from the day she starts working there. That means an Italian contract, Italian payroll and Italian social security registration. A US payroll run will not cover it.

There are two ways to handle this. You can set up a local company, or you can use an Employer of Record (EOR). A local company means incorporation, a bank account, a payroll provider and a legal representative. An EOR hires Dana in Italy on your behalf, while you direct her daily work. For a single relocation, the EOR route is usually faster and cheaper to run.

## The paperwork before she lands

Dana is a US citizen, so she needs the right to live and work in Italy before she starts. This is the longest and least predictable part. Do not book flights until the permit path is clear.

In parallel, the employer side prepares:

- The employment contract under the applicable national collective agreement.
- Registration with the Italian social security institutions.
- Payroll setup, including the tax withholding that Italy requires from the employer.

Dana also needs an Italian tax code. She will need it for almost everything: the bank account, the lease, the phone contract.

## Arrival and start date

Once the permit is in place, Dana arrives and signs her documents. The EOR confirms her start. From this day, payroll runs monthly in Italy.

Help with housing and registration at the local registry office makes the first weeks easier. Dana is more productive when the basics are handled. Plan for this support. It is part of the relocation, not an extra.

## Reading the first Milan payslip

The first payslip surprises many US managers. In the US, you see gross pay, a few taxes and net pay. An Italian payslip lists many lines. Some are paid by Dana, and some are paid by the employer on top of her gross salary.

One line is specific to where she lives: the municipal income tax surcharge, called the *addizionale comunale*. Each city sets its own. Milan applies a single rate of 0.8%. According to the source we checked, it also has an exemption threshold of EUR 23,000.

In practice:

- The surcharge is calculated on taxable income, not on the whole cost of employing her.
- Below the EUR 23,000 threshold, the surcharge does not apply.
- Dana's senior salary will likely be above that threshold, so expect to see the line on her payslip.
- The rate is small next to the other deductions, but it is a real line item. Dana should know it is coming.

One caution. The figures above come from a secondary source, not from the city itself. Municipalities can change their rules. Ask your payroll team to confirm the rate and how the threshold is applied before you quote net pay to Dana. Do not promise her a net figure based on a general estimate.

The surcharge is also not always collected in a simple monthly amount. Italian payroll often spreads local surcharges across the year and settles them at year end. Your payroll provider can show you how it appears in the first months. Dana should not read an early payslip as the final picture.

## What to tell Dana before the first payday

A short briefing prevents most questions:

- Gross and net pay will differ more than she is used to.
- Her payslip will include national and local taxes, including the Milan surcharge.
- The employer pays additional contributions that she will not see as deductions.
- Net pay can change slightly during the year as withholding adjusts.

Share a sample payslip with her early. It removes anxiety and builds trust with the new employer setup.

## What to tell finance

Finance cares about total cost, not net pay. The cost of Dana is her gross salary plus employer contributions, plus the EOR fee, plus relocation support. The Milan surcharge is paid out of her income, so it does not add to your cost directly. It does affect what she takes home, and that affects how she judges the offer.

Build the model from gross salary upward. Then check net pay with Italian payroll before you finalize the offer letter.

## The short version

Decide on the employing structure first. Clear the permit path before booking travel. Set up Italian payroll before the start date. Explain the payslip, including the Milan surcharge, before the first payday. Verify local rates with your payroll team and not with a blog post, including this one.

Do this in order and the first Milan payslip will hold no surprises.
