# Minimum Daily Earnings for Italian Social Security: Why They Matter for Part-Time Roles

> Italy sets a minimum daily earnings figure for social security. Here is why low or part-time pay can still trigger minimum contribution bases.



Many US teams assume social security costs scale down in step with pay. If an Italian employee works part time, the thinking goes, the employer cost shrinks in proportion. In Italy that is not always true. A floor applies, and it can lift the contribution base above what the salary alone would suggest.

## What the floor is

Italian social security (INPS) uses a minimum daily earnings figure. It acts as a lower limit for the earnings on which contributions are calculated. The figure published by a secondary source is 58.13 EUR per day. It is derived as 9.5% of 611.85.

The idea is simple. Contributions are not calculated on whatever the contract happens to say if that amount falls below the floor. They are calculated on the floor.

## Why this matters for part-time roles

Part-time and low-paid roles are the cases where the floor is most likely to bite. A US company might hire someone for a few hours of support work, a short weekly schedule, or a small retainer-style role. The pay looks modest. The budget looks modest too.

The floor can change that picture. If the daily pay in the contract sits below the minimum daily earnings, the contribution base does not follow the pay down. It stops at the minimum. The employer then owes contributions on a base that is higher than the actual wage.

This means the cost of a very small role does not fall to near zero. There is a baseline cost that comes with having an employee on the payroll at all.

## Where finance teams get surprised

Three patterns come up often.

**Pro-rata budgeting.** A finance team takes the full-time cost and cuts it by the share of hours worked. That works for salary. It can understate social security if the floor applies.

**Low salary, assumed low burden.** A role is priced as cheap because the gross pay is small. The contribution base may be set by the floor, not by the pay.

**Comparing contractors and employees.** A founder compares a small contractor invoice with a small employment contract. The employment contract carries contributions that the invoice does not. The floor makes the gap wider at the low end.

## What HR and legal ops should check

Before you offer a part-time or low-paid role in Italy, run through a short list.

- Convert the contract pay into a daily figure and compare it with the minimum daily earnings.
- Confirm which contribution rules apply to the role and the sector.
- Check whether the employer cost still makes sense once the floor is applied.
- Review the figure against the current INPS publication before you commit. The number above comes from a secondary source, and INPS is the authority on it.

## The practical takeaway

In Italy, part-time does not automatically mean proportionally cheaper. For roles with very low pay, the minimum daily earnings can set the contribution base. That changes the real cost of the hire.

The fix is not complicated. Model the cost with the floor included from the start. A small role should have its own calculation, not a scaled-down copy of a full-time one.

This also helps with expectations inside your company. When finance sees the floor in the model, a higher cost for a small role is no longer a surprise. It is a known line item.
