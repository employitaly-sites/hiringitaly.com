# Onboarding Compliance Checklist for a New Italian Employee

> A practical checklist of the mandatory compliance steps for onboarding a new employee in Italy during the first month, from hiring notice to safety training.



Hiring in Italy does not end when the employee signs. The first month carries a set of mandatory steps. Some must happen before the start date. Others must happen right after. Missing them can lead to fines and disputes later.

This checklist covers what US founders, HR, finance and legal ops teams need to track. Exact deadlines depend on the contract type and the collective agreement, so confirm them with your local payroll or legal provider.

## Before the start date

### Confirm the right to work

For EU citizens, check identity documents and the tax code. For non-EU citizens, you need a valid work authorization or visa before the person starts. Do not let anyone begin work while the permit is pending.

### Choose the collective agreement

Most Italian employment is governed by a national collective labor agreement (CCNL). It sets minimum pay, working hours, leave, notice periods and extra monthly payments. Pick the one that matches your sector and role. Everything else in the contract depends on this choice.

### Issue the employment contract and hiring information

The employee must receive written information on the key terms of the job. This includes job title, grade, pay, working hours, workplace, probation period, notice and the applicable CCNL. Give it in writing, in a language the employee understands. Many teams provide Italian and English versions.

### File the hiring notice

The employer must notify the authorities of the new hire before the employment begins. This is done through the mandatory online communication system. It is not optional and it cannot be fixed casually after the fact. Late or missing notices are a common source of penalties.

## At the start date

### Register with social security and insurance

The employer registers the employee with the national social security institute and the workplace injury insurer. These registrations drive contributions, sickness benefits, parental leave and injury cover. Payroll cannot run correctly without them.

### Collect the data payroll needs

Gather the tax code, bank details, address, family situation for tax deductions, and any prior employment details. Ask early. Missing data is the most frequent cause of a wrong first payslip.

### Deliver the privacy notice

Italian employers must give the employee a data protection notice. It explains what personal data you collect, why, and for how long. If you use monitoring tools, laptops with tracking, or location features, say so clearly. Remote monitoring of staff is restricted in Italy, so check the rules before you switch anything on.

## During the first month

### Health and safety obligations

This is where foreign employers are often caught off guard. Italian law places heavy duties on the employer for workplace safety, even for office and remote staff.

- Give general safety training and role-specific training.
- Make sure a risk assessment document exists and covers the role.
- Check whether the role needs a medical fitness check by a company doctor.
- Provide required protective equipment and ergonomic setup for screen work.

Keep signed records of every training session. Inspectors ask for proof, not promises.

### Remote and hybrid work agreements

If the employee works from home, sign a written agreement on remote work. It should cover equipment, working hours, the right to disconnect, and safety rules for the home workspace. A verbal arrangement is not enough.

### Set up time and leave tracking

Italian rules on working hours, rest periods and overtime are strict. Set up a way to record hours and leave from day one. Define how holiday, permits and sick leave are requested and approved. Tell the employee how to report sickness and who to notify.

### Handle the probation period correctly

If the contract has a probation period, it must be written in the contract and signed before work begins. Verbal agreements do not count. Track the end date so you can act on time if the role is not working out.

### Run the first payroll with care

Review the first payslip line by line. Check the base pay, the extra monthly payments required by the CCNL, tax withholding, and employer and employee contributions. Fix errors immediately. Early mistakes tend to repeat.

## A simple tracking approach

Turn the list into a shared checklist with an owner and a status for each item. Split it into three groups: before start, start date, and first month. Review it weekly until every line is closed.

Keep a file for each employee with the signed contract, the hiring notice receipt, the privacy notice, the training records and the remote work agreement. If an audit or dispute comes, this file is your defense.

## When you have no Italian entity

If your company has no Italian entity, you cannot file these notices or register with the institutions on your own. An Employer of Record (EOR) acts as the legal employer and handles each step above. You direct the work. The EOR carries the compliance duties.

Whether you use an EOR or your own entity, the principle is the same. Do the legal steps in the right order, and keep proof of each one.
