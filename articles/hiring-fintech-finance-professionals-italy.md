# Hiring Fintech and Finance Professionals in Italy: Who They Are and Where to Find Them

> A practical profile of Italian fintech and finance talent, where these people work and gather, and how US teams can reach and hire them.



If you are building a finance or fintech team in Italy, start with a clear picture of the talent. Italy has a deep pool of finance professionals. Most of them were trained in a banking, insurance or professional-services setting. That shapes how they work and what they expect from an employer.

This article covers the typical profiles, where they are based, where to find them, and what to plan for when you hire.

## The talent profile

Italian finance talent tends to fall into a few groups.

**Bank and insurance veterans.** Italy has a large traditional banking and insurance sector. Many experienced people spent years in risk, compliance, credit, treasury or actuarial work. They know regulation well. They are often strong on process and documentation. They may be less used to the pace of a venture-backed product team.

**Big-firm alumni.** Audit, tax and advisory firms train many accountants and finance analysts. These people are rigorous and used to client work. They are a good fit for finance operations, controllership, reporting and FP&A.

**Fintech and payments builders.** Italy has a growing fintech scene. Engineers, data people and product managers work in payments, lending, digital banking and insurtech. Many have worked at startups or at international companies with Italian offices.

**Regulatory and compliance specialists.** Italian financial rules sit inside the wider European framework. Specialists in anti-money-laundering, payments regulation, data protection and supervisory reporting are a real strength of the market. If your product needs a local compliance lead, this is a useful pool.

## Where they are based

Milan is the center of gravity. Banks, insurers, asset managers, consultancies and most fintech companies concentrate there. If you need depth in a niche, Milan gives you the widest choice.

Rome matters for people close to public institutions, supervisors and large corporates. Turin, Bologna and Trieste have strong banking and insurance roots, and they often have talent that is less contested than in Milan.

Remote work has widened the map. Many finance and engineering professionals now work from smaller cities or from home. A candidate who lives outside Milan may still be open to a remote role with a US company. Do not limit your search to one city unless the job needs on-site presence.

## Where to find them

**LinkedIn.** It is the main channel for professional roles in Italy. Search in both English and Italian job titles. Many Italian professionals list their role in Italian only, so an English-only search misses people.

**Universities and business schools.** Bocconi in Milan is the best-known source of finance graduates. Politecnico di Milano is a strong source for engineering and data talent in fintech. Other universities across the country feed regional banking and insurance employers. Early-career hiring through these networks works well if you can offer training.

**Professional bodies.** Accountants and auditors belong to professional registers and local associations. These groups run events and have member directories. They are a useful route to senior finance and tax people.

**Fintech communities.** Milan hosts meetups, accelerators and industry events. Attending or sponsoring a local event puts you in front of people who are already open to change.

**Referrals.** Italian professional circles are close. A recommendation from a respected person carries weight. Once you have one good hire, ask for introductions.

**Former employees of competitors.** Large banks and insurers release talent regularly. People leaving a big institution for a smaller, faster company are common. Approach them with a clear pitch about scope and ownership.

## What to plan for

**Language.** Many finance professionals speak good English, especially in Milan and in international firms. Fluency still varies. Test it in the interview if the role needs daily English with your US team. Also decide whether the role needs Italian for local filings and authorities.

**Contracts and collective agreements.** Italian employment is shaped by national collective agreements. The sector you hire into can change the pay structure, leave and notice terms. Check which agreement applies before you fix an offer.

**Notice periods.** Senior candidates often have long notice periods with their current employer. Build that into your hiring timeline.

**Total compensation.** Candidates look at the full package, not only base pay. Benefits, extra monthly payments and job security matter to them. A clear explanation of the package helps you compete with banks that offer stability.

**Career path.** People from large institutions want to know where the role leads. Be specific about growth, ownership and who they will report to.

## Hiring without a local entity

You do not need to set up a company in Italy to hire here. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ your hire locally, run payroll, handle contributions and keep the contract compliant, while you direct the daily work. This is the fastest route for a first hire or a small team, and it lets you test the market before committing to an entity.

## Practical steps

Define the role and the regulatory knowledge it needs. Search in Italian and English. Use Milan as your base for niche skills, but stay open to remote candidates. Check the collective agreement early. Plan for notice periods. Explain the full package.
