# Relocation Checklist for Employees Moving to Italy

> A practical checklist for employees moving to Italy: housing, tax code, bank account, healthcare registration and schools, in the order that works.



Moving an employee to Italy goes well when the steps happen in the right order. Many of them depend on each other. A bank wants a tax code. Healthcare registration wants proof of residence. Residence wants an address. If one step slips, the rest stall.

This checklist covers the five areas that matter most: housing, tax code, bank, healthcare and schools. Use it as a working plan for the employee and for your HR and finance teams.

## 1. Housing

Housing comes first because almost everything else needs an address.

- **Start with a short-term stay.** Book temporary housing for the first weeks. This gives the employee time to see neighborhoods before signing a long lease.
- **Expect a formal lease.** Italian rental contracts follow set rules. The employee should get a written, registered contract. Ask the landlord or agent to confirm the registration.
- **Check what is included.** Utilities, building fees and furnishings vary a lot. Ask in writing what the rent covers.
- **Keep the paperwork.** The lease is the main proof of address for the steps below. Keep a copy ready.

If the employee is bringing family, factor in space and school location before choosing the area.

## 2. Tax code (codice fiscale)

The codice fiscale is the personal tax identification number. Nearly every formal act in Italy needs it: opening a bank account, signing a lease, activating utilities, registering for healthcare, and being set up on payroll.

- **Get it early.** Some people can request it before arrival through an Italian consulate. Others get it from the tax authority after landing. Check which route applies to the employee's situation.
- **Bring valid ID.** A passport is the base document. Visa or permit documents may also be needed.
- **Do it for family too.** Spouses and children need their own codice fiscale for healthcare and school.
- **Send it to payroll.** Your payroll provider needs the number to run the first salary correctly.

This is the step that unlocks the rest. Make it the first task after arrival, or before it if possible.

## 3. Bank account

An Italian account makes daily life easier. Salary, rent and utilities usually run through it.

- **Bring the standard documents.** Expect to show a passport, the codice fiscale, proof of address and often the employment contract or permit.
- **Compare account types.** Traditional banks and online banks both operate in Italy. Online banks can be faster to open. Branch banks may handle in-person needs better.
- **Ask about the language.** Not every branch works in English. Check before the appointment, and bring a translator if needed.
- **Plan for a gap.** Opening an account can take time. Make sure the employee has a way to pay for rent and living costs in the meantime, such as a foreign card or a relocation advance.

Finance teams should confirm the account details early so the first payroll run is not delayed.

## 4. Healthcare registration

Italy has a national health service. Registering gives access to a family doctor and public care.

- **Register with the local health authority.** This is usually done at the local office covering the employee's address. Residence status and permit type decide the route and the cost.
- **Choose a family doctor.** After registration, the employee picks a general practitioner. This doctor is the entry point for referrals and prescriptions.
- **Keep the health card.** The card is used for visits, tests and pharmacies.
- **Consider private cover.** Many employees add private insurance for faster access to specialists or English-speaking doctors. Check what your benefits plan offers.

Until registration is complete, the employee needs a bridge. Travel or private insurance can fill that gap.

## 5. Schools

Families with children need to plan schooling as early as housing.

- **Choose the type of school.** Options include public schools, private schools and international schools. Each has different language, curriculum and cost.
- **Check the enrollment process.** Public school enrollment follows a national procedure and calendar. Arriving mid-year can limit choices. International schools set their own rules and may have waiting lists.
- **Prepare records.** Bring school reports and ID. Some documents may need translation or official recognition.
- **Think about language.** A child who does not speak Italian will need support. Ask each school how it helps new students.
- **Link school to housing.** Public school placement often depends on where the family lives. Settle the address first.

## The order that works

1. Secure temporary housing, then a lease.
2. Obtain the codice fiscale for every family member.
3. Register residence at the local municipality.
4. Open the bank account.
5. Register with the health service and choose a doctor.
6. Enroll children in school.

Residence registration at the municipality sits between the lease and healthcare. Do not skip it. It confirms the employee lives at the address and feeds into other registrations.

## What employers can do

You do not need to run every step yourself. You do need to know who owns each one. Assign a clear owner for housing, documents, payroll data and family needs. Give the employee a written list with the order above. Check progress at set points, so small delays do not turn into missed payroll or gaps in coverage.

A clear plan reduces stress for the employee and reduces risk for your company.
