# Mid-Month Start in Italy: How the First Paycheck Works

> An employee joins mid-month in Italy. See how pay is prorated, when the first payslip arrives and what the new hire should expect to see.



A new hire starts in the middle of the month. Payroll has to pay for part of that month only. This is common, and it is easy to plan for once you know the pattern.

This article walks through the scenario for US teams. It covers proration, payslip timing and what your employee should expect.

## Proration: pay for the days worked

In Italy, monthly pay is tied to the calendar month. When someone starts partway through, payroll pays only for the part of the month that falls inside the employment relationship.

The employee does not get a full month of salary. They also do not wait a full month for their first payment. They get a partial amount that reflects the start date.

How the partial amount is calculated depends on the national collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to the role. Some agreements set how a day is valued. Others leave less room for interpretation. Your payroll provider applies the right method for the contract in question. You should ask which method is used and have it confirmed in writing before the start date.

## What shapes the partial amount

A few things change the number on the first payslip:

- The agreed gross salary and how it is spread across the year.
- The exact start date.
- Whether the contract treats certain days in the month as paid or unpaid.
- Any allowances or benefits that begin on the start date.
- Employee social contributions and income tax withholding, which are calculated on the partial gross.

The last point matters. Because the gross is smaller, the deductions are smaller too. The net amount is lower than a normal month, but the ratio between gross and net stays in line with the rest of the year.

## Thirteenth and fourteenth month pay

Many Italian contracts include additional monthly payments that build up over the year. They are earned in proportion to the time worked. A mid-month start means the first period counts partially toward these amounts.

The employee will not usually see a separate payment for this on the first payslip. The amount accrues and is paid later, at the time set by the applicable agreement. It is worth explaining this to the new hire early. Otherwise they may think something is missing.

## When the first payslip arrives

Italian payroll works by month. The payslip is produced after the month closes, once attendance data is final. Salary is then paid on the date set by the contract or the collective agreement. This is often at the end of the month or shortly after it.

For a mid-month starter, this means the first payment can feel late. The employee may wait close to a full month after their start date before money arrives. That is normal in Italy, but it surprises many US hires who are used to biweekly pay.

The payslip itself usually comes as a PDF, delivered by the payroll provider or through a portal. Paper copies are less common now.

## What to tell the employee before day one

Set expectations in the offer or onboarding call. Cover these points:

- The first payment will be partial.
- It will arrive after the month closes, not at the end of the first pay period.
- The payslip will show gross pay, contributions, tax withholding and net pay.
- Extra monthly payments accrue and are paid later.
- Who to contact with questions about the payslip.

This short briefing prevents most early support requests.

## What you need to send payroll

Payroll cannot run without clean inputs. Send these before the cutoff for the month:

- The confirmed start date.
- The signed contract and the applicable collective agreement.
- The employee's tax code and bank details.
- Any one-time items, such as a relocation allowance or a sign-on payment.

Missing data is the main cause of a delayed first payslip. If the start date shifts after payroll has closed, the correction usually lands in the next cycle.

## Common mistakes

**Quoting a full month of net pay.** Tell the candidate the first month will be lower.

**Promising a payment date you cannot control.** Payment follows the payroll calendar, not the start date.

**Forgetting registration steps.** The employer must register the new hire with the relevant authorities before work begins. Late registration creates problems that go beyond payroll.

**Mixing up gross and net.** Italian offers are usually stated as gross annual salary. The employee will see a much lower net figure, and that is expected.

## Why an Employer of Record helps

With an Employer of Record (EOR), the provider is the legal employer. It handles registration, contract terms, payroll and payslips. You pay a single invoice that reflects the employee's cost. The employee gets a compliant payslip on the Italian schedule, and you do not need a local entity or payroll team.

You still own the start date and the data. Send both early, and the first payslip will be right.
