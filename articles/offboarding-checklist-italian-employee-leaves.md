# Offboarding an Italian Employee: A Checklist for US Employers

> A practical offboarding checklist for US companies when an Italian employee leaves: equipment return, final pay, access removal and required documents.



When an employee in Italy leaves, the exit is not just an IT ticket and a farewell lunch. Italian rules shape how you end the relationship, what you owe in the last payslip and which papers you must hand over. US teams often treat offboarding as a quick admin step. In Italy, mistakes here can turn into disputes months later.

This checklist covers four areas: the exit decision, equipment return, final pay and access removal, and documents. Use it as a working list, and confirm each point against the employee's contract and the national collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to them.

## Start with the reason for the exit

The steps depend on how the employment ends. A resignation, a dismissal, a mutual agreement and the end of a fixed-term contract each follow different rules. Notice periods, formal communication and the right to challenge the exit vary by type.

Before anything else:

- Confirm the exit type and who initiates it.
- Check the notice terms in the contract and the CCNL.
- Send all formal communication in writing and keep proof of delivery.
- Do not announce the exit to the team until the paperwork is settled.

If you plan a dismissal, talk to an Italian employment lawyer or your Employer of Record (EOR) before you say anything to the employee. An informal remark in a call can create problems later.

## Equipment return

Set the return process up early. Do not wait for the last day.

- List every item issued: laptop, phone, monitor, badge, keys, tokens, company card.
- Match the list against your asset register and the signed handover record from the start of employment.
- Arrange prepaid shipping or a courier pickup for remote staff.
- Ask the employee to confirm in writing what they return and the condition of each item.
- Wipe and re-image devices only after you have copied any business data you need.

Be careful with deductions. Do not take the cost of unreturned equipment out of final pay on your own. Italian law limits what an employer can withhold from wages. Raise the issue separately, or agree on a settlement in writing.

Also check personal data on company devices. If the employee used a work phone for private matters, handle that data with care and follow your privacy policy.

## Final pay

The last payslip is where most errors happen. Final pay in Italy usually has several parts, and each one needs a check.

- Salary for the final period worked.
- Any accrued but unused holiday and leave.
- Any prorated share of additional monthly salary payments that your CCNL provides, such as a thirteenth month.
- Pay in lieu of notice, if it applies to this type of exit.
- Variable pay, bonuses or commissions that have been earned under the plan rules.
- The end-of-service payment known as TFR (trattamento di fine rapporto).

TFR deserves its own line in your checklist. It builds up during employment and is paid when the relationship ends. Your payroll provider should calculate it from the employee's history. Confirm whether any part has been moved to a pension fund, because that changes what you pay out directly.

Ask payroll for a draft final statement before the last day. Review it with the employee's manager. Then agree on the payment timing and keep a copy of the calculation.

Expense reports are the last piece. Collect outstanding claims and reimburse them with the final pay, so nothing stays open.

## Access removal

Access removal should happen on the exit date, and in some cases earlier. Plan it by system, not by person.

- Email, calendar and chat accounts.
- Single sign-on and password managers.
- Code repositories, cloud consoles and admin panels.
- Shared drives, CRM and finance tools.
- Bank and card access, including company cards.
- Customer-facing accounts and shared mailboxes.
- Building access, if the employee used an office or coworking space.

Transfer ownership before you delete anything. Reassign files, recurring meetings, customer accounts and open tasks to a named colleague. Set an automatic reply on the mailbox for a defined period, then close it according to your retention policy.

Italian privacy rules and workplace monitoring rules apply to mailbox access. Do not read a former employee's mailbox without a documented reason and a clear internal procedure. Ask your EOR or counsel how to handle this before you need to.

## Documents

Finally, the paperwork. Some documents go to the employee. Others go to Italian authorities. Your EOR or payroll provider normally prepares these, but you should know what is on the list.

- The final payslip and the TFR statement.
- A work certificate that states the period of employment and the role.
- Any tax documents the employee needs for their annual filing.
- The required notifications to the relevant authorities about the end of employment.
- Signed acknowledgment of returned equipment.
- If you agreed a settlement, the signed agreement, ideally made in a form that protects it from later challenge.

Keep your own file too. It should include the exit communication, the final pay calculation, proof of payment, the equipment record and the access removal log. Store it according to Italian retention rules, not your US defaults.

## A simple order of work

1. Confirm the exit type and notice terms.
2. Ask payroll for the draft final statement.
3. Send the equipment return instructions.
4. Plan access removal by system and name a successor for each.
5. Prepare the documents and the notifications.
6. Run the exit day: collect items, close access, pay.
7. Archive the file.

A clean exit protects the employee's trust and your legal position. It also keeps the door open if you want to rehire or ask for a reference later.
