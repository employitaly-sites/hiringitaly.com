# Non-Compete Clauses in Italy: What Makes Them Enforceable

> US non-competes rarely survive in Italy as written. See what Italian law requires: written form, limits on scope, and real pay for the restriction.



If you hire in Italy and bring your US template, expect trouble. Italian law does not ask whether a non-compete is reasonable in the abstract. It asks whether the clause meets fixed statutory conditions. Miss one, and the clause is void.

## How the US approach differs

In most US states, a non-compete is a contract term like any other. A court asks if it protects a legitimate interest and if its scope is reasonable. Consideration is usually the job itself or a signing bonus. Some states ban these clauses almost entirely. Others enforce them broadly. Courts in many states will narrow an overbroad clause and enforce the rest.

Italy works differently. The Civil Code sets the conditions. The parties cannot waive them. A clause that fails is null, and a court will not rewrite it into something valid. There is one partial exception on duration, covered below.

## What a valid Italian non-compete needs

### 1. A written agreement

The clause must be in writing. A verbal promise or a policy handbook line will not do. Put it in the employment contract or in a separate signed agreement.

### 2. Limits on the activity covered

The restriction must be tied to the work the employee actually does for the employer. It cannot stop the person from earning a living in their field. A clause that blocks any job in the whole industry is likely void. Draft it around the specific products, services or functions where the employee holds sensitive knowledge.

### 3. A defined territory

The geographic scope must be reasonable and stated. A worldwide ban for a mid-level employee who only served Italian clients invites a challenge. Match the territory to where the business really competes and where the employee had exposure.

### 4. A time limit set by law

The statute caps how long the restriction can run after employment ends. The cap is longer for executives than for other staff. If your clause runs past the cap, the duration is cut back to the legal maximum. This is the one place where Italian law saves part of a clause. Do not count on it. Draft within the cap from the start.

### 5. Real compensation

This is the requirement that surprises US teams most. The employee must receive a specific payment for accepting the restriction. It is separate from salary for the work. The amount must be proportionate to the sacrifice. A token sum is treated as no payment, and the clause fails.

Courts look at how wide the restriction is, how long it lasts, and what the employee loses. A broader and longer restriction needs higher pay. There is no fixed formula, so the amount is judged case by case.

The payment can be structured in different ways, such as during employment or after it ends. Whatever you choose, it must be identifiable as the price of the non-compete. If it is buried inside a general pay increase with no label, you invite a dispute.

## What applies even without a clause

During employment, a duty of loyalty already applies. The employee cannot work for a competitor or use company information against the employer. You do not need a non-compete for that. The clause matters only for the period after the job ends.

## Common mistakes by US employers

- **Copying a US template.** Clauses that lean on a choice of foreign law do not override Italian mandatory rules for an employee who works in Italy.
- **No separate payment.** Employment itself is not consideration here.
- **Overbroad scope.** Industry-wide and worldwide bans are the usual reason clauses are struck down.
- **Adding the clause late.** It is safer to agree it at hiring, with proper pay, than to ask for it mid-employment.
- **Assuming customer non-solicits are safe.** A broad ban on contacting clients can be treated as a non-compete and tested against the same rules.

## Practical steps

1. Decide if you need the restriction at all. For many roles, confidentiality terms and the duty of loyalty give enough protection.
2. If you do need it, define the activity, territory and duration narrowly and in writing.
3. Set a payment you can defend as proportionate, and label it clearly.
4. Have Italian counsel review the final text before the employee signs.
5. Budget for the payment. It is a real cost that belongs in your hiring model.

## Using an employer of record

When you hire through an employer of record, the Italian entity is the employer on paper. The clause is signed with that entity. Make sure the restriction reflects the business interest you actually need to protect, and that the cost of the payment is part of the agreed fee structure from the start.
