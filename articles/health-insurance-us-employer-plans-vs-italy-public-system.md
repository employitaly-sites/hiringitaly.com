# Health insurance in Italy: why US-style benefits budgets shift to other perks

> US employer health plans do not map onto Italy's public system. See why benefits budgets in Italy move toward other perks, and what to plan for.



If you run a US company, health insurance is probably the largest line in your benefits budget. It is also the benefit candidates ask about first. In Italy, the picture is different. Understanding why will save you money and avoid a bad first offer.

## The US model: the employer is the safety net

In the US, most people get coverage through their job. The employer picks a carrier, negotiates a plan, pays a large share of the premium, and answers questions when a claim goes wrong. Candidates compare plans the way they compare salaries. A weak plan is a reason to say no.

This makes health cover a core part of total compensation. It also makes it a cost you carry for every hire.

## The Italian model: the state is the safety net

Italy has a national public health service. It covers residents, including employees, and it is funded through general taxation and payroll contributions. An employee in Italy does not need an employer plan to see a doctor, go to a hospital, or get care for a serious illness.

This changes the starting point. Your new hire is not asking, "Will I have any coverage?" They already do. Your employer plan is not a lifeline. It is an add-on.

## What the public system does not do well

The public system is broad, but people complain about it in familiar ways. Waiting times for some specialist visits and tests can be long. Access and quality vary by region. Some people want a private room, a chosen specialist, or faster scheduling.

That is where supplementary private cover comes in. Many Italian employees value it as a way to skip queues and widen choice. It is a convenience and comfort benefit, not a basic need.

## Collective agreements already shape the offer

Many Italian employees work under a national collective agreement for their sector. Many of these agreements include some form of supplementary health fund, with contributions set by the agreement. Your company may be required to follow that structure, and it may already cover part of what a US plan would cover.

This matters for two reasons. First, you cannot treat health benefits as a blank page. Second, if you design a rich US-style plan on top, you may pay twice for overlapping cover.

Check the agreement that applies to your sector before you design any package. It will tell you what is already included.

## Why budgets shift to other perks

When the state covers the basics and the collective agreement covers part of the rest, the marginal value of a big employer health plan drops. Candidates notice other things instead.

Finance and HR teams that hire in Italy tend to move money toward benefits that the employee feels every month. Common examples:

- Meal vouchers, which employees use daily.
- Commuting support or transport benefits.
- Flexible work arrangements and remote days.
- Extra paid leave beyond the legal minimum.
- Training and learning budgets.
- Family and childcare support.
- Retirement savings contributions.
- Wellness stipends for gym or therapy.

Some of these have tax or contribution treatment that makes them efficient for both sides. A euro spent in the right form can reach the employee with less leakage than a euro of plain salary. Your payroll provider or adviser should confirm the treatment for each perk before you commit.

## What to tell your US leadership

Expect questions from the US side. A CFO may ask why you are not matching the US plan. A founder may worry that the Italian team will feel second class.

Here is a plain answer. Italian employees already have universal coverage. Matching the US plan would add cost without adding what candidates value. The better move is to keep what the collective agreement requires, add supplementary cover only where it helps you hire, and put the rest of the budget into perks people use.

## A practical way to decide

Work through these steps for each role:

1. Identify the collective agreement that applies and list what it already provides for health.
2. Decide whether supplementary private cover is a hiring need for this role or a nice extra.
3. Ask candidates what they value. In Italy, the answer is often about time, flexibility, and day-to-day support.
4. Price each perk, including employer contributions and any tax treatment.
5. Set a total benefits budget per head and allocate within it.

Senior hires and people relocating from abroad may ask for private cover more often. They may be used to it, or they may want care in English. Treat these as individual requests and price them as such.

## Do not forget expats and families

If you move a US employee to Italy, health coverage needs separate thought. Residency status, registration with the public system, and family members all affect what applies. Do not assume the US plan follows the person. Sort this out before the start date, not after the first doctor visit.

## The bottom line

In the US, health insurance is the center of the benefits package. In Italy, it is one piece among many, and often a smaller one. The public system carries the base. The collective agreement adds a layer. Your budget is best spent where your team will notice it.

Plan the package by role, check the applicable agreement, and keep the offer simple. You will spend less on duplicated cover and more on what helps you hire and keep people.
