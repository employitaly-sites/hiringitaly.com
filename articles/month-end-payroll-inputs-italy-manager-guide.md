# How to Run Month-End Payroll Inputs for an Italian Team

> What a manager must send to payroll each month for an Italian team: overtime, absences and expenses, how to send them, and when. Plain steps, no jargon.



Payroll in Italy runs on inputs. The payroll team does not guess what happened in your month. It processes what you send. If an input is late or unclear, the payslip is wrong or delayed, and the employee notices.

This guide covers what a manager must send, what each input needs to say, and when to send it.

## Why the timing matters

Payroll closes on a fixed calendar. After the cutoff, the data is locked and checked. A change that arrives later does not vanish. It moves to the next cycle as a correction. Corrections cost time, confuse employees and create extra work for finance.

Ask your payroll provider for the cutoff calendar at the start of the year. Put the dates in your team's shared calendar. Treat the cutoff like a board deadline.

## The three inputs

Most months, three things change a payslip: overtime, absences and expenses. Everything else is usually stable. Start with these.

### 1. Overtime

Overtime in Italy is governed by the law and by the national collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to the employee. The agreement decides what counts as overtime, how it is paid and what limits apply. So you cannot copy your home-country rules.

Send these details for each employee:

- The dates worked.
- The hours worked beyond the normal schedule.
- Whether the hours fell on a night, a Sunday or a public holiday.
- Who approved the hours, and when.

Get approval before the work, not after. Payroll will ask for it. A short email from the manager is enough if your policy allows it. Do not send a round total with no dates. Payroll cannot apply the right rate to a total.

If the employee prefers time off instead of pay, say so. Some agreements allow it. Payroll needs to know which route you chose.

### 2. Absences

Absences are the input with the most variety. Each type is treated differently for pay and for social contributions. Common types are:

- Paid vacation.
- Paid leave for permits.
- Sickness.
- Maternity and paternity leave.
- Unpaid leave.
- Public holidays that fall on working days.

For each absence, send the employee name, the type, the first and last day, and any document that supports it. Do not label sickness as vacation to keep things simple. The two have different effects on pay and on state benefits.

Sickness has its own rules. The employee must report it, and a medical certificate is filed through an electronic system. Your role as manager is to tell payroll the days off as soon as you know them. Do not wait for month-end to mention a long illness.

For planned absences, send them as soon as they are approved. Payroll can then track balances all year. At month-end you only confirm.

### 3. Expenses

Expense reimbursements need clear support, because the tax treatment depends on what the cost was and how it was documented.

For each claim, send:

- The employee name.
- The date and the business purpose.
- The amount and the currency.
- The receipt or invoice, readable and complete.
- Any travel details, such as the destination and the reason for the trip.

A receipt alone is not enough. A meal receipt with no purpose may be treated as pay. Ask employees to add a one-line note when they submit. As manager, approve the claim before it reaches payroll. Payroll checks the paperwork. It does not judge whether the spend was needed.

Send all claims for the month together, with one list. Do not send them in pieces across several days.

## A simple monthly routine

Use the same rhythm every month. Routine removes most errors.

- **Near the end of the month:** remind your team to submit hours, absences and expense claims.
- **Before the cutoff:** review each item. Check dates, approvals and receipts.
- **At the cutoff:** send one consolidated file to payroll. Use a shared template, so fields are always in the same place.
- **After processing:** read the draft payroll summary if you receive it. Look for anything unexpected, such as a missing overtime payment or a wrong absence.

Keep a copy of what you sent. If an employee asks a question about a payslip, you can answer from the record.

## Common mistakes

- Sending hours without dates.
- Mixing absence types.
- Approving overtime after the fact.
- Sending receipts with no business purpose.
- Sending changes after the cutoff and expecting them to be in the same payslip.
- Using several channels, such as chat, email and calls, with no single source of truth.

## Who owns what

The manager owns the facts: who worked, who was absent and what was spent. Payroll owns the calculation and the filings. Finance owns the budget view. When each side keeps to its role, the cycle is short and quiet.

If you employ people in Italy through an Employer of Record, the provider runs payroll and filings for you. You still send the same three inputs. The provider tells you the cutoff and the format, and you meet them.
