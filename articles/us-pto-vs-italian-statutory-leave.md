# Paid Time Off in Italy: US PTO Policies vs Statutory Leave

> US PTO is a company benefit. In Italy, paid leave is a legal right set by statute and collective agreements. What that means for your Italian hires.



In the US, paid time off is a benefit. You design it, you cap it, and you can change it for the next hiring cycle. In Italy, paid leave is a right. The law sets the floor, and national collective agreements often add to it.

This difference shapes how you plan, budget and manage an Italian team.

## How PTO works in the US

Most US employers run a single PTO bank. Vacation, sick days and personal days sit in one pool. The employer picks the accrual rate, the carryover rules and the cap. Some companies offer unlimited PTO. Many states do not require payout of unused balances at termination, and some do.

The result is flexibility for the employer. It is also a culture where taking leave can feel optional. Employees often leave days unused. Managers often sign off on time off as a favor.

## How leave works in Italy

Italy does not treat leave as a single bank. The law and the applicable national collective agreement (CCNL) define separate entitlements:

- **Annual vacation.** A statutory minimum applies to every employee. The CCNL for the sector may give more. Part of the leave must be taken within the year it accrues. The employer cannot replace that part with a cash payment while the contract is running.
- **Public holidays.** These are set nationally. They are paid and they do not count against vacation.
- **Extra paid permits.** Many CCNLs add paid hours or days off on top of vacation. These are usually tracked separately from vacation.
- **Sick leave.** Illness is a separate category with its own rules. It is not drawn from vacation. Pay during sickness is split between the employer and the social security body, depending on the contract and the length of the absence.
- **Family and parental leave.** Maternity, paternity and parental leave are protected by statute. Other family-related permits are also protected.

The employee does not need to "earn" these in the way a US accrual plan works. They are part of the employment relationship.

## Where US teams get caught out

**You cannot copy your PTO policy.** A US policy that offers a generous bank but no separate sick or holiday rules does not map onto Italian law. If your offer letter contradicts the CCNL, the CCNL usually wins.

**Unlimited PTO does not work.** Without tracking, you cannot show that the employee took the legally required leave. You also lose control over how much leave accrues and what happens at the end of the contract.

**Leave is not a favor.** Employees are entitled to take it. Your role is to schedule it, not to approve whether it happens. Italian law and the CCNL set rules on notice and on the employer's right to set the timing. The employer may have a say on when, but not on whether.

**Unused leave becomes a liability.** Accrued but untaken leave is a cost. If it is not managed, it can turn into payout obligations and, in some cases, penalties.

**Sick leave is not a PTO bucket.** Do not ask an employee to "use a PTO day" when they are ill. That is a US habit with no place in Italy.

## What this means for your budget

The cost of an Italian employee includes the time they are paid but not working. A US finance team that budgets only on salary will underestimate the real cost per working hour. The gap is not a surprise once you plan for it. It becomes a surprise only when the model assumes a single PTO bank.

Also plan for coverage. Many Italian businesses slow down in the summer, and many employees take a long block of leave at the same time. If your team in the US works through August, plan handovers early.

## A practical approach

Start with the CCNL that applies to the role. It tells you the vacation entitlement, the extra permits, the sick pay rules and the notice periods. Then build your internal policy around that, not the other way around.

Keep three things in place:

1. A clear record of leave taken and leave accrued for each employee.
2. A calendar process for planning vacation well ahead of peak periods.
3. Offer letters and handbooks that describe Italian entitlements in Italian terms, not US PTO language.

If you hire through an Employer of Record (EOR), the EOR applies the correct CCNL, tracks balances and handles payroll treatment for each category. You keep the day-to-day management. The EOR keeps the leave rules and the records correct.

## The cultural point

US managers sometimes read Italian leave habits as low commitment. That reading is wrong. Employees are using a right that the law protects. A team that takes its leave and returns rested is the normal outcome the system is built for.

Set expectations early. Tell your Italian hires that you understand the local rules and that you plan work around them. It builds trust faster than any perk.
