# From One to Five Employees in Italy: How Costs and Admin Grow in Year One

> How payroll cost and admin load grow as a US startup goes from one to five hires in Italy, and which recurring items to plan for at each stage.



Most US startups enter Italy with one hire. A sales lead, an engineer, a country manager. By the end of the year, the plan often says five.

The cost per person does not change much as you grow. The admin does. This article walks through what grows, what stays flat, and where teams get surprised.

## The first employee: everything is new

Your first hire in Italy sets up the whole structure. Someone has to register the employment, open the social security position, set up payroll and handle the insurance against workplace injury.

The cost is mostly fixed work, not fixed money. You pay for setup effort once, and it feels heavy because it is spread over a single salary.

Two employer costs matter from day one.

- **Social security (INPS).** For the pension branch, called IVS, the employer share is 23.81% of taxable pay, according to a secondary source. This is only one part of the employer contribution load. Other contributions come on top, so do not use this figure as your total.
- **Workplace injury insurance (INAIL).** The 2019 tariffs are still in force. The way they are applied changed with an update dated April 2026 and published in June 2026. Your rate depends on the type of work, so an office role and a field role will not cost the same.

With one employee, you can do all of this by hand. It is slow, but it works.

## The second and third employees: the routine starts

The second hire is cheaper to onboard than the first. The pension contribution is a percentage, so it scales with each salary. The setup you did for the first person is reused.

What you gain in repetition, you lose in variety. Two people can have different roles, different working patterns and different insurance risk. The injury insurance rate is tied to the work performed, so a mixed team may need more than one classification.

At three employees, monthly payroll becomes a real process. You need a calendar. Contribution payments, payslips and reporting all follow a regular cycle. Missing one is not a crisis, but it creates follow-up work.

This is also where founders notice that the admin does not shrink. It repeats.

## The fourth and fifth employees: complexity, not cost per head

By the time you reach four or five people, your payroll cost is simple to forecast. Take salary, add the employer contribution load, add the injury insurance. The pension share of 23.81% applies to each person's taxable pay in the same way.

The harder part is variety. A larger team means:

- more starting dates and contract changes to track,
- more leave, absences and adjustments to process each month,
- more questions from employees about payslips and benefits,
- more chances that a rule or rate changes during the year.

The INAIL change is a good example. The tariffs themselves stayed in force, but the way they are applied was updated. A team with one hire may not notice. A team with five has more positions to review and more to correct if something was applied the old way.

## What grows and what stays flat

| Item | How it behaves as you grow |
|---|---|
| Pension contribution | Grows with each salary, same percentage |
| Injury insurance | Grows with payroll, rate depends on the work |
| Setup effort | Heavy once, then reused |
| Monthly payroll work | Grows with every added person |
| Monitoring rule changes | Same effort, but more people are affected |

The pattern is clear. Money scales in a straight line. Admin scales in steps, with a jump at the start and smaller increases after.

## Where startups get surprised

Three things come up again and again.

**Using one number for the whole cost.** The 23.81% pension share is not the full employer cost. Teams that budget on one contribution figure end up under-budgeted. Build the cost from every component.

**Treating setup as finished.** Rules and rates get updated. A rate you checked early in the year may be applied differently later. Plan a regular review.

**Hiring faster than the process.** If hires arrive in quick succession, onboarding steps get skipped or done late. Fix the sequence once, then repeat it for every person.

## A simple way to plan the year

Start with the first hire and write down every recurring task. Then multiply the payroll items by headcount and leave the setup items alone. That gives you a realistic view of how monthly work will grow.

Do this before you commit to the fifth hire. You will know what your monthly routine looks like, and you can decide whether to run it in-house or hand it to a partner.

If you hire through an Employer of Record (EOR), the structure above still applies. The difference is who carries the recurring work. The cost items are the same. The admin sits with the provider instead of your finance team.

## The short version

Money grows with headcount. Admin grows in steps. The first hire is the hardest to set up. The fifth is the hardest to keep consistent.

Plan the recurring work early, build costs from every component, and review rates during the year.
