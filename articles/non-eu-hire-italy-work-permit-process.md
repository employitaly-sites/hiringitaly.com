# Bringing a Non-EU Hire to Italy: The Work Permit Process Step by Step

> How a sponsored non-EU hire moves from quota and work authorization to visa and residence permit in Italy, in the order each step happens.



You found the right person. They live outside the EU. Before they can start in Italy, three separate documents have to line up: the work authorization, the entry visa and the residence permit. Each one is issued by a different office. Each depends on the one before it.

This guide walks through the sequence for a sponsored hire.

## The order at a glance

1. Check whether a quota slot exists for the role and the worker's nationality.
2. The employer applies for work authorization (the *nulla osta*).
3. The worker applies for the entry visa at an Italian consulate.
4. The worker arrives and signs the stay contract.
5. The worker applies for the residence permit.

Skip or reorder any of these and the file stalls.

## Step one: check the quota

Most sponsored non-EU hires depend on the annual entry quotas, known as *flussi*. The current framework is the DPCM of 02/10/2025, which covers the 2026-28 period.

For 2026, the non-seasonal quota is 43300 entries. It is split by category:

- 25000 for nationals of the countries listed in article 6, paragraph 2, letter a.
- 18000 for nationals of countries with cooperation agreements.
- 300 for stateless persons and refugees.

The worker's nationality decides which bucket applies. A candidate from a country with no agreement and not on the list may have no path through the quota at all. Check this before you make an offer, not after.

Some categories sit outside the quotas. We have not verified the current rules for seasonal work or for out-of-quota routes, so confirm those with an adviser before you rely on them.

## Step two: the employer applies for the nulla osta

The employer, or its Italian representative, files the request online with the Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione. This is the one-stop immigration desk at the local prefecture.

Expect to provide:

- The job offer, with role, pay and working hours.
- Proof of the employer's capacity to pay the salary.
- Proof of suitable housing for the worker.
- The collective agreement (CCNL) that governs the role.

A foreign company with no Italian entity cannot easily act as the sponsor. This is where many founders get stuck. The sponsor must be an employer with a legal presence in Italy. An Employer of Record can fill that role while you build your own entity, or instead of building one.

If the file is approved, the desk issues the *nulla osta*. This is the permission to bring the worker in. It is not yet a visa.

## Step three: the visa

With the *nulla osta* in hand, the worker applies for a work visa at the Italian consulate in their country of residence. The consulate checks the *nulla osta*, the passport and the supporting documents. It may also ask for proof of the worker's qualifications.

A few practical points:

- The visa application is personal. The worker must usually appear in person.
- The visa is tied to the approved job. It does not allow a change of employer or role.
- Documents issued abroad often need translation and legalization. Start those early.

When the visa is granted, the worker can travel to Italy.

## Step four: arrival and the stay contract

After entry, the worker and the employer go back to the immigration desk. There they sign the stay contract (*contratto di soggiorno*). It confirms the employment terms that the authorization was based on.

At this point the worker also needs an Italian tax code (*codice fiscale*). It is required for the contract, for payroll and for the permit application.

The employer must also notify the start of the employment through the mandatory online communication. Your payroll provider normally handles this.

## Step five: the residence permit

The visa only gets the worker through the border. To stay and work, they need the residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*). They must apply within the legal deadline after arrival. The application is filed through the postal kit system, followed by an appointment at the police headquarters (*Questura*) for fingerprints and identity checks.

Until the card is printed, the receipt from the application serves as proof that the process is under way. In practice, the card arrives well after the worker has started. Plan for that gap in your onboarding and your payroll set-up.

## Where files fail

Most delays come from the same few causes:

- Applying in the wrong quota category.
- Housing or income evidence that does not match the offer.
- A job description that differs between the application and the contract.
- Missed deadlines after arrival.

Keep the paperwork consistent from the first form to the last. Every document is checked against the others.

## What to decide first

Before you start, settle three questions. Who is the sponsoring employer in Italy? Which quota category does the candidate fall into? Who owns the deadlines after arrival?

If you can answer all three, the rest is a checklist.
