# Budgeting for Workplace Injury Insurance in Italy: Office Roles vs. Field Technicians

> Why an office role and a field technician carry different INAIL injury premiums in Italy, and how to budget for each before you hire.



If you hire in Italy, workplace injury insurance is not a flat add-on. It depends on what the person actually does. A finance analyst at a desk and a technician who installs equipment at customer sites will not cost you the same.

This article explains why, and how to build the line into your hiring budget.

## Who provides the cover

In Italy, workplace injury and occupational disease cover runs through INAIL, the national insurance body. Employers pay the premium. The employee does not. It is mandatory, and it applies from the first day of work.

For a US team, the closest comparison is workers' compensation. The logic is similar. Higher-risk work costs more to insure.

## How the premium is built

The premium is not set per person. It is set per type of work. Each activity is classified under a rate table, and the rate is applied to the payroll of the people doing that activity.

The table in force is the 2019 tariff. The rules for applying it changed in 2026, so older guides may be out of date. We cover what changed below.

In practice, three things drive the final number:

- The classification of the work the person performs.
- The payroll amount for that person.
- The employer's own injury record over time.

The first one is where role type matters most.

## Office role: low exposure

A person in finance, HR, sales operations or software development who works at a desk sits in a low-risk classification. Injuries are rare and usually minor. The rate reflects that.

For budgeting, treat this as a small line item. It will not move the total cost of an employee in any major way.

## Field technician: higher exposure

A field technician drives to sites, handles tools and equipment, and works in environments you do not control. Injuries are more likely and can be more serious. The classification is higher, and so is the rate.

The same salary can therefore produce a noticeably larger insurance cost for a technician than for an office employee. The gap comes from the rate, not from the pay.

## Mixed roles need care

Many roles are not clearly one or the other. Think of a sales engineer who spends part of the week in an office and part at customer plants. Or a project manager who visits construction sites.

Classification follows the real activity. It does not follow the job title. If you label someone "account manager" but they spend most days on a production floor, the risk profile is not that of an office worker. Describe the actual duties when you set up the role. A wrong classification can lead to corrections later, and corrections cost more than getting it right at the start.

## What changed in 2026

There is one update worth knowing. The 2019 tariff stays in force, but the way it is applied was modified in 2026, with the measure published in June.

Two points matter for planning:

- A new bonus table now links the reduction to the number of worker-years and to an accident index known as ISAR.
- For insurance positions that are not statistically significant, the fixed reduction is now 13%. It was 5%.

The second point mostly affects small positions, where there is too little data to measure the employer's record in a meaningful way. If you start with a small team in Italy, this may apply to you.

Do not build your budget on the reduction, though. Whether it applies depends on your insurance position and your record, and a new team has no record yet. Budget at the full rate first. Treat any reduction as a later saving.

## How to budget by role

A simple approach works well.

1. List each planned role and describe the real daily work, including travel and site visits.
2. Mark each role as office, field, or mixed.
3. Ask for the classification that fits the real activity, not the title.
4. Apply the rate to the expected payroll for that role.
5. Keep the reduction out of the base case.

Then review the numbers when the team grows. A company that starts with office staff and later adds field technicians will see its average insurance cost per head rise. That is normal. It is better to expect it than to find it in a quarterly review.

## Why it matters for the business case

Injury insurance is only one part of employer cost in Italy. It sits next to social security contributions, severance accrual and other items. But it is the one that changes most with job content rather than with salary.

This is why a flat percentage on top of salary can mislead. If your plan mixes desk roles and field roles, model them separately. The result is closer to what you will actually pay.

## Summary

- Injury premiums depend on the type of work, not the job title.
- Office roles carry low rates. Field technicians carry higher ones.
- Mixed roles follow their real activity.
- The 2019 tariff is in force, and the 2026 changes affect how reductions work.
- Budget at the full rate and treat reductions as upside.
