# Offsites and Team Travel with Italian Employees: Policy, Per Diems and Planning

> How to plan offsites and business travel for Italian employees: write a clear travel policy, handle per diems and expenses, and avoid payroll surprises.



Bringing your Italian team to an offsite is a good idea. It builds trust and shortens decision cycles. But travel for Italian employees is not the same as travel for contractors or US staff. Pay, rest, expenses and payroll treatment all follow Italian rules and your employment contracts.

This guide covers what to settle before you book anything.

## Start with a written travel policy

Most problems come from unwritten expectations. A manager says "just expense it" and later finance disagrees. Put the rules on paper before the trip.

A workable policy answers these questions:

- Who approves a trip, and by when?
- What counts as business travel, and what counts as commuting?
- Which costs are paid directly by the company, and which are reimbursed?
- What proof is needed for each expense?
- How are travel days treated for working time?

Keep it short and in plain language. Provide it in Italian as well as English. Employees should be able to read it without asking for help.

## Travel time and working time

Travel for work is not the same as the daily commute. If you ask an employee to travel to an offsite, travel time can raise working time questions. Rest periods also matter. A late return followed by an early meeting is a risk.

Your national collective agreement may add rules on travel, transfers and overtime. These differ by sector. Do not assume your US policy fits. Ask your payroll provider or employer of record to check the agreement that applies to each employee before you finalize the schedule.

Plan the agenda with this in mind. Avoid starting early on the day after a long trip. Leave room for rest. It also makes for a better offsite.

## Per diems and reimbursements

There are two common ways to cover costs on a trip: reimbursement of actual expenses, and a fixed daily allowance. Each has a different payroll and tax treatment in Italy.

With reimbursement, the employee submits receipts. The company pays back documented costs. This is simple to explain and easy to audit. It needs good receipts and a clear process.

With a fixed daily allowance, the employee gets a set amount per day of travel. This is easier to administer, but the tax treatment depends on the rules that apply and on how the allowance is structured. Part of an allowance may be treated as taxable pay in some cases. Do not promise a net amount to the employee until payroll has confirmed it.

Whichever you choose, apply it consistently. Mixed approaches across the team create disputes and may create compliance gaps.

Ask payroll to confirm these points in writing:

- Which method you will use for each trip type
- What documents must be kept
- How the payment will appear on the payslip
- Whether any part is taxable or subject to contributions

## Domestic trips versus trips abroad

An offsite in Italy and an offsite abroad can be treated differently for allowances and documentation. If the team travels outside Italy, check whether any different rules apply to allowances. Also check whether employees need travel documents, and whether insurance covers them in the destination.

If you invite employees from several countries, do not copy one country's policy to the others. Each location has its own rules.

## Insurance and safety

Business travel brings the question of coverage if something goes wrong. Confirm that employees are covered while traveling for work. Check what your own travel or health policy covers. Share emergency contacts and a simple plan for who to call.

Also think about duty of care at the event itself. Plan transport after evening events. Offer alternatives for people who prefer not to join late activities.

## Be careful with side costs

Offsites often include meals, activities, gifts and shared accommodation. Some of these may be treated as benefits in kind for tax purposes. Gifts and entertainment need particular care. Ask payroll before you promise anything beyond the core travel costs.

Also consider personal circumstances. Some employees have caregiving duties or health needs. Give notice early, offer flexibility, and be clear about what is optional.

## A simple planning timeline

Work backward from the event date.

- **Early planning:** set the purpose and budget, and confirm the policy with payroll.
- **Mid planning:** announce dates, share the policy, and collect traveler details.
- **Close to the event:** book travel, confirm the agenda, and brief managers on rules for hours and expenses.
- **After the event:** collect receipts quickly, submit expenses to payroll on time, and review what worked.

Late expense claims are a common source of payroll errors. Set a clear deadline and enforce it kindly.

## What to do next

Before you plan the next offsite, send your draft policy to your payroll team or employer of record. Ask them to mark anything that does not fit Italian rules or the applicable collective agreement. Fix those points now, not after the trip.
