# How to Onboard an Italian Employee Remotely: A Day-by-Day First-Week Plan

> A practical day-by-day plan for onboarding a new hire in Italy remotely, from pre-start paperwork to the first Friday check-in.



A remote hire in Italy can feel settled by Friday or lost by Wednesday. The difference is usually preparation. This plan gives you a day-by-day structure for the first week, plus what to finish before Monday.

## Before Monday: close the gaps

Most first-week problems start before the first day. Check these items by the Friday before the start date.

- The signed employment contract is in the employee's hands, in Italian, with an English copy if they want one.
- The mandatory registrations with the Italian authorities are filed. Confirm this with your payroll or Employer of Record (EOR) partner. Do not assume it happened.
- The applicable national collective agreement (CCNL) is identified. It drives leave, notice, and many pay details.
- Laptop, accounts, and a password manager invite are ready.
- The employee has a named buddy and a named manager.

Send a short welcome note with the schedule for the week. People relax when they know what comes next.

## Monday: arrival and access

Keep Monday light. The goal is that the employee can log in and knows who to ask.

- Start with a video call from the manager. Cover the week's plan, not the company strategy.
- Confirm that the equipment works and every account opens.
- Walk through the payroll and benefits basics. Explain how payslips arrive and who to contact with questions.
- Share the privacy notice and any data protection forms. Italy takes employee data seriously, so handle this on day one.
- End the day with a short check-in. Ask what was confusing.

## Tuesday: role and people

Tuesday is about context.

- Give a clear picture of the role. Write down what success looks like at the end of the first month.
- Introduce the team in small video calls, not a single large one.
- Pair the employee with the buddy for an informal hour. Let them ask basic questions without feeling judged.
- Share the team calendar, meeting rhythm, and where decisions get made.

If your team is mostly in the US, agree on overlap hours today. Italy is many hours ahead of US time zones, so set a window that works for both sides and write it down.

## Wednesday: tools and first task

By midweek, the employee should do real work.

- Run a hands-on session on your core tools and workflows.
- Assign a small, finishable first task with a clear owner and deadline.
- Cover the health and safety basics that apply to the role, including home workstation guidance for remote staff. Your EOR partner can tell you what training is required and how to deliver it.
- Review the company handbook. Flag where local rules differ from your home-country defaults, such as working time and leave.

## Thursday: expectations and compliance habits

Thursday is for the conversations that are easy to skip.

- Hold a first one-to-one with the manager. Review goals and how feedback will work.
- Explain how to request leave and report sickness. Italian practice has its own steps, so give written instructions.
- Cover working hours and how you will respect time off. Be explicit that no one is expected to answer messages late in the evening.
- Explain expense rules and how reimbursement works.

Write these down. A short page in the shared drive beats a long call that nobody remembers.

## Friday: review and next steps

Close the week with a feedback loop.

- Review the first task together. Praise what worked and be specific about what to change.
- Ask the employee for honest feedback on the onboarding. What was missing? What was too much?
- Set goals for the following weeks and book the recurring one-to-ones.
- Confirm that all signed documents are returned and filed.
- Send a brief summary of the week to the employee, the buddy, and your HR or finance contact.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Treating the hire like a contractor.** An Italian employee has statutory rights and a defined contract. Do not run onboarding as if it were a freelance engagement.
- **Skipping the local paperwork check.** Late or missing registrations create problems that are hard to fix later.
- **Overloading Monday.** Too many meetings leave the new hire tired and silent.
- **Forgetting the language.** Provide key documents in Italian. Offer English summaries where helpful.
- **No named point of contact.** Remote staff need one person who answers fast.

## Who owns what

Split responsibilities clearly. The manager owns the role and the work. The buddy owns the small questions. HR or your EOR partner owns contracts, registrations, payroll, and compliance. Finance owns the budget and the cost view. When each person knows their part, nothing falls between the cracks.

A strong first week does not need to be complicated. It needs a plan, a calendar, and people who show up. Follow the structure above and your new hire in Italy will know the job, the team, and the rules by Friday.
