# Where to Find Tech Talent in Italy: Channels That Actually Work

> Job boards, communities, universities and referrals compared. A practical guide for US teams hiring engineers in Italy.



Hiring engineers in Italy is not hard because talent is scarce. It is hard because US teams look in the wrong places. The channels that work in San Francisco or New York do not always work in Milan, Turin or Rome.

This guide compares four channels: job boards, communities, universities and referrals. Each has a clear use. Each has a clear limit.

## Job boards

Job boards are the default starting point. They are fast to set up and they reach a wide audience.

**LinkedIn** is the main board for mid-level and senior engineers. Most Italian tech professionals keep a profile there. Recruiters from large firms and startups all post there, so you compete for attention. Expect many applicants and a lot of noise.

**Local boards** reach different people. Italian engineers also browse national job sites and tech-specific boards. Some candidates never update LinkedIn but do check these sites.

**What works:** A post written in both English and Italian. A clear salary range. A named hiring manager. Italian candidates often distrust postings that hide pay or the real employer.

**What fails:** A generic US job description with no mention of location, contract type or working hours. Candidates want to know if the role is remote, hybrid or on-site. They also want to know who employs them.

**Best for:** Volume hiring and roles with clear titles, such as backend, frontend or QA.

## Communities

Communities are slower, but the quality is higher. Italy has active developer groups, meetups and online forums. Many cover specific stacks like Python, Rust, JavaScript, data engineering or DevOps.

You can find people through:

- Local meetups in larger cities
- Slack and Discord groups for specific languages
- Open source projects with Italian maintainers
- Conferences and hackathons

The rule is simple: contribute before you recruit. Answer questions. Sponsor an event. Share a technical blog post from your team. Members notice when a company only shows up to post a job.

**Best for:** Niche skills, senior engineers and passive candidates who are not actively applying.

**Limit:** It takes months to build a reputation. This channel does not fix an urgent vacancy.

## Universities

Italy has strong technical universities, and several produce graduates with solid engineering fundamentals. Polytechnic schools in Milan and Turin are well known for this. Many other universities have good computer science and engineering programs.

How to work with them:

- Contact career offices directly
- Offer internships that can convert to full-time roles
- Give a guest lecture or run a workshop
- Sponsor a student project or competition

Universities are the best source of junior talent. Graduates are often eager to join international teams and learn English-speaking workflows.

**Limit:** Juniors need mentoring. If your Italian team will be one or two people, you may not have the capacity to train. Also, a US company with no local presence can look unfamiliar to career offices. Be ready to explain who you are and how the hire will work.

**Best for:** Junior hires and a steady pipeline over time.

## Referrals

Referrals are often the best channel for quality. They work well in Italy because professional networks are tight. Engineers know who is good, and they know who is looking.

Start with your first hire. A strong first engineer in Italy will bring in others. Give them a clear process for introducing people. Make the interview fair and fast. Reward referrals in a way that fits local norms, such as a bonus paid after the new hire completes the probation period.

**What works:** Asking your existing Italian team, advisors and investors for introductions. Asking engineers in other countries if they know Italians who want to work with you.

**Limit:** Referrals depend on who you already have. If you have no one in Italy, you have no network to tap. They can also narrow diversity if you rely on them alone.

**Best for:** Senior roles and cultural fit after the first hire is in place.

## How to combine them

No single channel is enough. A practical mix looks like this:

1. **Start with a job board** to get the first applicants and test the market.
2. **Join two or three communities** in your stack while the search runs.
3. **Hire the first engineer** and ask them for referrals.
4. **Add university contacts** once you need junior roles and have someone to mentor them.

This order matches how trust builds. Boards give speed. Communities give reputation. Referrals give quality. Universities give a pipeline.

## The employer question

Candidates will ask who their employer is. A US company without an Italian entity cannot easily hire locally, and many engineers will hesitate to join without a proper Italian contract, local payroll and social contributions. Be ready with a clear answer before the first interview.

An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person in Italy on your behalf while you direct the daily work. This lets you hire without setting up a company first. Explain this early. It removes a common reason for candidates to drop out.

## Practical tips

- Write job posts in English and Italian.
- Publish the pay range and contract type.
- Reply to applicants quickly. Slow answers lose good people.
- Offer a technical interview in English, with an Italian-speaking interviewer if possible.
- Explain benefits in local terms. Italian employees expect things like paid leave and a statutory end-of-service payment.

## Summary

| Channel | Speed | Quality | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job boards | Fast | Mixed | Volume, standard roles |
| Communities | Slow | High | Niche, senior talent |
| Universities | Medium | Good for juniors | Junior pipeline |
| Referrals | Medium | High | Senior, cultural fit |

Pick the channels that match your timeline and your seniority needs. Then be clear about who the employer is and how the contract works.
