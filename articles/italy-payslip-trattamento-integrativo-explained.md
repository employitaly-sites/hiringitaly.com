# The Low-Income Tax Supplement on Italian Payslips: What the Line Means

> Employees on lower salaries in Italy see a line called trattamento integrativo. Here is what it is, why it appears, and what employers should tell staff.



If you hire in Italy, sooner or later an employee on a lower salary will ask about a payslip line called *trattamento integrativo*. It is a tax supplement for low earners. It adds money to the net pay. It is not a bonus from you, and it is not a discretionary benefit.

This article explains what the line is, why it shows up, and how to answer the question without a call to your accountant.

## What the line is

Italian income tax (IRPEF) is withheld from salary each month. For people on lower incomes, the law adds a supplement on top of the net pay. On the payslip it appears under the name *trattamento integrativo*. Some payroll systems label it with a short form or a code, but the idea is the same.

The supplement is capped. The verified figure we use here is a maximum of 1,200 euros per year. It applies to employees with income up to 15k euros, and only under conditions. The conditions matter. Not everyone below the income threshold receives the full amount, and some receive nothing.

Because the source for this figure is a secondary one, treat it as a guide to the order of magnitude. Your payroll provider should confirm the exact rules for each employee.

## Why employees ask about it

There are three common reasons.

**It is a positive line.** Most payslip lines reduce pay. This one adds to it. People notice, and they want to know whether it will continue.

**It can change month to month.** The supplement depends on the employee's income and tax position. If pay changes, the supplement can change too. A new hire who starts mid-year, or someone who works fewer hours, may see a different amount than a colleague.

**It can disappear.** If an employee's income rises above the limit, or the conditions are no longer met, the line goes away. That can look like a pay cut when it is not one. The gross salary is the same. The supplement just no longer applies.

## Who pays for it

The employee receives it through payroll, so it is the employer who pays it out with the salary. In practice the employer then recovers the amount by offsetting it against the taxes and contributions it already owes the state. So the supplement should not be a net cost to you, but your payroll team has to handle it correctly.

This is one reason to work with a payroll provider that knows Italian rules. A wrong calculation means either an underpaid employee or an amount that has to be corrected later.

## What happens at year end

The supplement is based on the employee's expected income for the year. Final eligibility is checked at year-end adjustment. If the employee turns out not to meet the conditions, the amount already paid may have to be returned, usually through the final payslips.

This surprises people. An employee who received the line all year may see a deduction later. It is worth telling staff in advance that the supplement is provisional until the year is closed.

This tends to happen when:

- the employee had other income during the year
- the employee started or left a job mid-year
- the pay was higher than first estimated

## How to explain it to an employee

Keep the message short. Something like this works:

> This line is a tax supplement the state gives to employees with lower income. It is added to your net pay. Whether you get it, and how much, depends on your income and your situation. It can change, and it is checked again at year end.

Then add what you cannot promise. You cannot guarantee the amount. You cannot guarantee it will stay on the payslip. You can only say that payroll applies the rule as written.

## What this means for your cost planning

If you are a US founder or finance lead, there are two practical points.

First, the supplement does not change the cost of the employee to you in a major way. It changes what the employee takes home. When you compare offers, look at the net pay and not only the gross salary. Two roles with the same gross can leave different amounts in the employee's pocket if one of them qualifies for the supplement.

Second, this affects how a raise lands. If a salary increase pushes someone above the income limit, part of the raise can be offset by losing the supplement. The employee may feel the raise is smaller than expected. Warn people before the change shows up on a payslip.

## What to check with payroll

When onboarding someone on a lower salary, ask your payroll team or provider these questions:

- Does this employee qualify, and on what basis?
- Is the line calculated correctly in the first payslips?
- How will year-end adjustment work if eligibility changes?
- What do we tell the employee in writing?

A clear answer to each one prevents most of the questions later.

## Summary

The *trattamento integrativo* is a tax supplement for lower earners. It adds to net pay. The cap we use is 1,200 euros per year, for income up to 15k euros, under conditions. It can change or stop, and it is verified at year end. Explain it early and keep the wording plain.
