# Why Italian Salaries Are Quoted Gross Annual (RAL) and How to Read Them

> RAL is the gross annual salary Italian candidates quote and negotiate on. Learn how to read it, how monthly installments work, and what it means for cost.



If you hire in Italy, you will hear the term RAL early. It stands for Retribuzione Annua Lorda. In English, gross annual salary. Candidates use it in interviews, on CVs and in offer talks. Almost nobody quotes a net monthly figure.

This article explains what RAL covers, why the market works this way, and how to avoid common misreads.

## What RAL means

RAL is the total gross pay an employee earns in a year, before income tax and before the employee's share of social security contributions. It is the contractual base number. It is not what lands in the bank account.

RAL usually does not include the employer's own costs. Employer social security contributions, severance accrual and insurance sit on top. So RAL is not the cost to the company either. It sits in the middle: higher than take-home pay, lower than total employer cost.

## Why Italy quotes gross

Three reasons explain the habit.

The national collective agreement for the sector sets pay levels in gross terms. Job grades, minimum pay and many allowances are written as gross amounts. Candidates compare offers against that frame.

Net pay is personal. It depends on family situation, tax deductions, regional and municipal surcharges and other individual factors. Two people with the same RAL can take home different amounts. Gross is the only figure that is comparable across people.

Gross is also stable. Tax rules and contribution rates change over time. A gross figure written in a contract stays clear even when the net result moves.

## Monthly installments are not always a simple split

US teams often divide the annual figure by the number of calendar months and call it the monthly salary. In Italy this can be wrong.

Many contracts pay the annual gross in more installments than there are calendar months. The extra installments are usually paid at set times of the year, often around the end-of-year holidays and in summer. The exact structure depends on the applicable collective agreement and the contract.

This matters for two reasons.

First, the monthly gross on a payslip can be lower than a simple division suggests. A candidate who says their RAL is a certain amount is describing the yearly total. The regular monthly payment is a fraction of that total, and the extra installments make up the rest.

Second, cash flow in the year is uneven for the employee and for the company. Payroll costs rise in the months when the extra installments are paid. Finance teams should model that in their forecast.

Always ask which installment structure the offer assumes. State it in the offer letter so there is no confusion.

## Why candidates negotiate on gross

Negotiating on net sounds simpler, but it shifts risk. If the employer promises a net figure, any change in tax or contributions becomes the employer's problem. Few companies accept that.

Negotiating on RAL keeps the conversation clean. The candidate asks for a gross number. The employer decides if it fits the budget. Net pay follows from the rules, and the candidate can estimate it with a simple tool.

It also helps to know that candidates often compare RAL across offers and against what peers earn. A gross figure that looks low next to the market will lose the candidate, even if the benefits package is strong. Benefits such as meal vouchers, health cover or a company car are usually discussed next to RAL, not inside it. Make clear whether a benefit is part of the gross figure or on top.

## How to read an Italian offer

Use this short checklist.

- Confirm the figure is gross annual.
- Ask how many installments the year is split into.
- Check which collective agreement applies and what it adds.
- Separate fixed pay from variable pay. A bonus is not part of the base unless the contract says so.
- List benefits and say if they are on top of RAL.
- Ask your payroll provider for the full employer cost, not only RAL.

## What this means for your budget

When a hiring manager says a candidate wants a certain RAL, that is only the start. The real cost to the company includes employer contributions, severance accrual, insurance and any benefits. Budgets built on RAL alone will fall short.

The reverse mistake is common too. Some founders convert RAL to a monthly figure, compare it with a US salary and conclude that Italy is cheap or expensive. The comparison only works when you place both on the same basis: total employer cost against total employer cost.

## Key takeaway

RAL is a gross yearly figure. It is the shared language of Italian hiring. Learn to read it, ask about installments and benefits, and always convert it to full employer cost before you decide.
